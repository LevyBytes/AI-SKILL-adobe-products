## Use thumbnail controls in the Project panel

### Use Premiere touch or mouse thumbnail controls to quickly play, scrub, and mark clips.

With thumbnail controls in Icon View, Premiere lets you scrub, play, and mark clips directly, without opening menus. The workflow supports touch gestures and mouse operation, reduces repeated navigation, and helps rough cuts and assembly edits move faster while keeping the controls visual.

Launch Adobe Premiere and open the project.

Select the Project panel (Shift+1).

Select Icon View.

Choose a clip so the thumbnail controls are shown.

Use buttons or drag on the thumbnail to scrub, play, or mark clips directly. During touch use, thumbnail controls appear automatically.

In the Project panel's Settings menu, select Thumbnail controls for all pointing devices when working with a mouse.

Tap the playback and scrub controls on the thumbnail to use touch gestures with the clip.

## Learn how touch and gesture actions work with buttons

### Navigate clips with touch gestures and buttons such as Frame Step, Shuttle, In/Out, speed drag controls, and drag-and-drop timeline edits, where Overwrite is the default.

Select the Frame Step button to move forward one frame; the arrow indicates the direction.

Drag to scrub at a slow rate, with speed increasing as high as 1x playback.

Select the Shuttle button during playback to double speed, up to 32x.

Drag to change speed between 1x and 32x; the arrow shows direction.

Select the In Point and Out Point buttons to set in/out points while playback is stopped or running.

Drag to scrub and set the point; the thumbnail updates.

Edit clips in the sequence.

Select-and-hold clip thumbnails, avoiding control buttons, then drag them to the Timeline or Program Monitor.

Note

Touch-only editing supports overwrite edits in the Timeline.

## Touch and Gesture controls on Windows Devices

### Use touch gestures on Microsoft Surface Pro and Windows devices to control Premiere for faster editing.

Premiere provides touch- and gesture-based editing for scrubbing, selecting, and arranging clips into rough cuts. The experience is tuned for touch, while thumbnail controls and Program Monitor drop-zones can also be used with a mouse. For best results, apply touch to assembly and rough-cut work, and switch to conventional mouse edits whenever needed.

On Microsoft Surface Pro and Windows multi-touch devices, control Premiere by touch. Combining gestures with keyboard work can speed up these activities:

- Build rough cuts
- Scrub through media
- Set in and out points
- Drag and drop clips onto timelines
- Edit clips

### One-finger tap/drag

With a one-finger tap, select a clip in the Project panel and show that clip's thumbnail controls. Use the thumbnail control's button, or drag the clip, to scrub. One finger can also drag the clip to the Program Monitor for edit drop-zones, or add it to the Timeline panel by dragging.

Use tap-and-drag on clips to preview, scrub, or add them straight to the timeline.

Use tap-and-drag on clips to preview, scrub, or add them straight to the timeline.

### Two-finger pinch to zoom

Pinching two fingers toward each other zooms a clip out. Moving two fingers apart zooms the clip in. You can zoom inside the Project panel's List View or Icon View, plus the Program Monitor panel and Timeline panel.

Pinch or spread two fingers on a clip while browsing in the Project panel to zoom out or in.

Pinch or spread two fingers on a clip while browsing in the Project panel to zoom out or in.

### Two-finger scroll

Use two fingers moving together in the same direction to scroll panels that have vertical or horizontal scroll bars. In panels with multiple scroll zones, such as the Timeline, scrolling follows only the direction and zone where the gesture started.

Swipe two fingers to move through clips in the Project panel vertically or horizontally.

Swipe two fingers to move through clips in the Project panel vertically or horizontally.

### Two-finger scrub

With a two-finger gesture similar to scrolling, scrub backward and forward through clips and sequences in Project panel Icon View and in the Program Monitor panels, much like hover scrubbing. The gesture moves the clip or sequence playhead in a 1:1 relationship to the thumbnail or monitor width, for durations up to 30 minutes. For each added 30-minute duration, perform another scrub gesture; after each gesture ends, the playhead stays at its stopping position.

To perform a two-finger scrub on a clip in the Project panel, tap the clip first to select it. The thumbnail controls are displayed.

Swipe two fingers horizontally across the thumbnail in either direction to scrub through a clip.

## Overview of workspaces

### Understand the Adobe Premiere home screen and workspace organization.

Premiere's main window is the Application Window. Inside it, panels are arranged into a Workspace. The default workspace includes panel groups as well as free-floating panels.

You can tailor a workspace by positioning panels in the layout that fits your editing style. When panels are rearranged, other panels resize to fit within the window. Save multiple custom workspaces for different tasks.

### Navigate the Home screen

The Home screen is the starting point in Premiere. From there, you can reach in-application tutorials, additional tutorials, and online documentation. The left panel lets you start a new project or open one that already exists.

Premiere Home Screen project creation and opening options.

Premiere Home Screen project creation and opening options.

Premiere adjusts the Home screen content over time. As you create more projects and gain experience, the screen can show more recent projects while reducing the amount of tutorial content.

### Workspaces

In Premiere, panels are arranged into layouts that can be stored as workspaces. Premiere includes 16 default workspaces, each shaped around post-production work such as color, audio, or graphics. Common general workspaces include:

- Essentials: suited to a single-monitor setup, with the primary editing controls kept close at hand.
- Vertical: built for vertical video workflows. Source Monitor and Program Monitor can be switched within the same panel.
- Learning: set up for using in-app tutorials and learning content while editing.
- Assembly: uses a large Project panel so rough cuts can be built quickly.
- Captions and Graphics: useful when the edit involves captions or graphics.
- Review: choose this workspace when Frame.io is part of your review process.
- Production: designed for team collaboration inside a Production.

## Import workspaces

### Importing a workspace inside an Adobe Premiere project.

By default, Premiere opens a project with the workspace that is currently active. You can instead have a project open with the workspace that was last used for that project, which helps when each project uses a different panel arrangement.

Launch Premiere.

Choose Window > Workspaces > Import Workspace from Projects.

Open the target project.

If an imported project opens with an empty workspace, close the project, turn off Import Workspace from Projects, import the project again, and then choose an existing workspace for that project.

## Dock, group, or undock panels

### Docking panels together, moving panels into or out of groups, and undocking panels so they float above the Adobe Premiere application window.

Docking zones: docking zones appear along the edges of a panel, group, or window. Dropping a panel on one of these zones places it beside the existing group and resizes the groups so the added panel fits.

Grouping zones: grouping zones appear in the center of a panel or group and along panel tab areas. To stack a panel with other panels, drag it onto a grouping zone.

### Dock or group panels

If the panel you need to dock or group is hidden, open it from the Window menu.

Move the required panel onto the desired drop zone. Premiere docks or groups it based on the kind of zone used.

Panels can be shown in either a stacked state or a tabbed state.

Hold Ctrl during the drag when the panel should float freely.

### Undock a panel in a floating window

A panel can be undocked into a floating window where panels can be added and changed, similar to the main application window. Floating windows are useful on a secondary monitor or when recreating layouts from earlier Adobe applications.

Choose the panel that should be undocked.

From the panel menu, select Undock Panel or Undock Panel Group.

Note

Another option is to hold Ctrl (Windows) or Command (macOS) while dragging the panel or group away from its current position. After you release the mouse button, the panel or group opens in a new floating window.

## Customize panels

### Opening, closing, scrolling panels, and changing the size of panel groups in Adobe Premiere.

### Open, close, and move panels

To open a panel, select Window, then choose the name of the panel you want.

Close a panel with Ctrl+W (Windows), Command+W (macOS), or the Close button.

To see every panel in a panel group, use the horizontal scroll bar to reveal all panel tabs.

To bring a panel forward within a panel group, select the panel tab you want in front, hover over the tab area, and drag tabs horizontally to reorder them.

Closing a panel group in the application window causes the remaining groups to expand into the freed space. Closing a floating window also closes the panels it contains.

### Resize panel groups

Resize icons appear when the pointer is placed over dividers between panel groups. Dragging those icons changes the size of every group that shares the divider.

For horizontal or vertical resizing, position the cursor between two-panel groups and drag the double arrow pointer.

Place the cursor where three or more panel groups meet and drag the four-way arrow to resize horizontally or vertically.

Note

Use multiple monitors when you need more screen area. A second monitor can show the video or the Premiere interface. Workspace settings store monitor configurations.

## Display any panel full-screen

### Expanding any panel into full-screen mode and toggling back to the normal view.

When editing in Premiere, you can expand a panel full-screen when your workflow needs extra room. The command can act on the panel with focus or on the panel under the mouse pointer.

Put the pointer over the panel to maximize.

Use the accent key (`) once to maximize the panel, then press it again to restore the panel size.

Tip

On many keyboards, this key is just left of the numeral one (1) key. It may also be described as the back quote key or the tilde key.

Another menu path is Window > Maximize Frame for the selected panel, followed by Window > Restore Frame Size when you need the previous size back.

For a computer with a non-U.S. keyboard, Adobe Premiere may bind Maximize or Restore Frame Under Cursor to something other than the accent key. The Keyboard Shortcuts dialog box shows the key assigned to Maximize or Restore Frame Under Cursor and to Maximize or Restore Active Frame. To find it, choose the Adobe Premiere Defaults set, choose Application from the menu, move past the Help heading, locate Maximize or Restore Frame Under Cursor, and assign the shortcut that works best.

## Tools panel in Premiere

### Options in the Tools panel in Premiere and how those features help editing workflows.

Choosing a tool changes the pointer shape to match that tool. For example, choosing the Razor tool and placing the pointer over a clip in the Timeline panel changes the pointer to a razor icon.

The Selection icon can also update to show the task currently in progress.

Note

Sometimes a modifier key, such as Shift, changes how a tool behaves while you use it, and the icon changes to match. Choose tools in the Tools panel, or trigger them with a keyboard shortcut.

By default, Premiere uses the Selection tool. It handles tasks that do not require a specialized tool. If Premiere is not behaving as expected, confirm that the Selection tool is active.

The Tools panel can also be resized and set to a vertical or horizontal orientation.

### Options in Tools panel

Premiere's Tools panel collects the editing tools used while working in the Timeline panel.

A. Selection tool B. Track Select tool C. Ripple Edit tool D. Razor tool E. Slip tool F. Pen tool G. Shape tool H. Hand tool I. Text tool J. Generative Extend tool

The Tools panel in Premiere provides access to the application's main edit tools.

Activate a tool for timeline work by selecting it directly or by pressing its keyboard shortcut. Hover over a tool to display its tool name and the matching shortcut.

- Selection: The regular choice for selecting clips, menu items, and other interface objects. After finishing with a specialized tool, switching back to Selection is usually the safest default.
- Track Selection: Use this tool when you need every clip after the cursor position in a sequence. Selecting one clip targets that clip plus later clips on the same track. Shift-clicking a clip expands the selection to later clips across all tracks. Holding Shift changes Track Selection into the Multi-track Selection tool.
- Ripple Edit: Use this tool to adjust a clip's In or Out point in the timeline. Ripple Edit removes any gap created by the trim while keeping edits on the affected side of the trimmed clip intact.
- Razor: Use this tool to cut clips in the timeline at one or more points. Select a location inside a clip to split it exactly there. Shift-clicking a spot in any clip cuts clips on every track at the same location.
- Slip: Use this tool to alter a clip's In and Out points together in the timeline while preserving the clip duration between them. For example, after trimming a 10-second clip down to 5 seconds in the timeline, the Slip tool lets you choose which 5 seconds are shown.
- Pen: Use this tool to create or select keyframes, or to modify connector lines in the timeline. Drag a connector line up or down to change it. Ctrl-click (Windows) or Command-click (macOS) a connector line to create a keyframe. Shift-click separate keyframes to select noncontiguous keyframes. Drag a marquee around adjacent keyframes to select a contiguous group.
- Shape: Use this tool when adding shapes to the sequence and timeline.
- Hand: Use this tool to pan the visible timeline area left or right. Drag left or right anywhere within the viewing area.
- Text: Use this tool when adding text to the sequence and timeline.
- Generative Extend: Use this tool to extend video and audio clips with Adobe's Firefly Video Model.

## Clip details in the Info panel

### Use the Info panel to review details for clips selected in the timeline.

For a selected item, the Info panel shows multiple data fields, along with timecode details for clips beneath the current-time indicator in the timeline.

The top area reports information for the active selection. Its contents change with the media type, the active panel, and related context. For instance, the Info panel shows different information for an empty area in the Timeline panel than it does for a clip in the Project panel.

- Video: reports frame rate, frame size, and pixel aspect ratio, in that order.
- Audio: reports sample rate, bit depth, and channels, in that order.
- Tape: shows the tape name.
- In: shows the selected clip's In point timecode.
- Out: shows the selected clip's Out point timecode.
- Duration: shows how long the selected clip lasts.

Below the current-selection data, the panel lists timecode values for the active sequence and for clips on each video and audio track. The stacked order mirrors the timeline, making the values easier to match visually. Video track timecodes place the highest track number at the top, while audio track timecodes place the highest track number at the bottom. This area is empty only when no sequences are open.

Adding or removing a track in the current sequence makes the Info panel refresh so the displayed track count stays accurate. The panel does not impose a display limit on track count. When the user changes to another sequence, the Info panel likewise refreshes to show that sequence's track total.

The Info panel provides timecode for the active selection and every track item under the current-time indicator. If the playhead moves across an empty part of the timeline, that track shows no timecode value, although the track label remains visible and not dimmed. The vertical timecode stack mirrors the sequence's track arrangement.

## Adobe Premiere FAQ

### Answers to common questions about working in Adobe Premiere.

Premiere is a nonlinear video editor built for flexible, customizable editing. It can import and combine many kinds of media, ranging from phone footage to raw 5K and higher resolution footage, and it lets editors work with that media in its native format instead of spending time on transcoding. The application includes a polished interface, an adjustable timeline, many editing shortcuts, and strong media-management features. Real-time playback matters for dense, complex sequences, so Premiere uses the Adobe Mercury Playback Engine to handle most formats and can use multiple GPU cards to shorten render and export work.

Because Premiere belongs to Adobe Creative Cloud, favorite settings, keyboard shortcuts, and workspace layouts can sync to any machine or edit bay. Creative Cloud membership also provides new updates and future releases as soon as they become available.

Premiere uses a scalable architecture, so faster systems can deliver stronger performance. Minimum system requirements vary with the media and workflow being used. Native 64-bit support allows Premiere to use all available system RAM. On multicore machines, Adobe recommends 4GB or more of RAM per core. The Adobe Mercury Transmit plug-in also improves integration with third-party I/O hardware.

Premiere works with a wide mix of hardware and software on macOS and Windows, allowing editors to choose workstations and capture cards that match their workflow and budget. Additional plug-ins can extend the system, including plug-ins from Adobe After Effects. Third-party hardware such as capture cards and decks is also supported.

Premiere handles standard-definition and high-definition formats from DV and HDV through HD and beyond. Some formats may still need additional hardware.
