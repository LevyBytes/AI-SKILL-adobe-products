# Enable glyph protection

Turn on Glyph protection in Adobe Photoshop so international fonts do not produce wrong or unreadable characters.

When a document uses fonts that are not available on your system, Glyph protection keeps text legible. After you choose a Roman font and then type non-Roman text, such as Japanese or Cyrillic, Photoshop can choose a suitable replacement font automatically so incorrect glyphs do not appear.

Adobe Photoshop turns glyph protection on by default to help international characters stay readable.

Open the Type preferences: Edit > Preferences > Type (Windows) or Photoshop > Preferences > Type (macOS).

Turn on Enable Missing Glyph Protection.

Enable Missing Glyph Protection: when checked, missing international fonts are substituted with suitable fonts.

Enable Missing Glyph Protection: when checked, unavailable international fonts are swapped for appropriate fonts.

Select OK to apply the changes.
