# Answers to common questions about working in Adobe Premiere.

Premiere is a nonlinear video editor built for flexible, customizable editing. It can import and combine many kinds of media, ranging from phone footage to raw 5K and higher resolution footage, and it lets editors work with that media in its native format instead of spending time on transcoding. The application includes a polished interface, an adjustable timeline, many editing shortcuts, and strong media-management features. Real-time playback matters for dense, complex sequences, so Premiere uses the Adobe Mercury Playback Engine to handle most formats and can use multiple GPU cards to shorten render and export work.

Because Premiere belongs to Adobe Creative Cloud, favorite settings, keyboard shortcuts, and workspace layouts can sync to any machine or edit bay. Creative Cloud membership also provides new updates and future releases as soon as they become available.

Premiere uses a scalable architecture, so faster systems can deliver stronger performance. Minimum system requirements vary with the media and workflow being used. Native 64-bit support allows Premiere to use all available system RAM. On multicore machines, Adobe recommends 4GB or more of RAM per core. Adding RAM can produce significant gains on any system, and 16GB of RAM is a practical baseline when several applications will run at once. The Adobe Mercury Transmit plug-in also improves integration with third-party I/O hardware.

Premiere works with a wide mix of hardware and software on macOS and Windows, allowing editors to choose workstations and capture cards that match their workflow and budget. Additional plug-ins can extend the system, including plug-ins from Adobe After Effects. Third-party hardware such as capture cards and decks is also supported.

Broad video-format support lets editors use the formats they need, including native editing for newer tapeless formats without transcoding or rewrapping. Premiere handles standard-definition and high-definition formats from DV and HDV through HD and beyond. Some formats may still need additional hardware.

Yes. Without After Effects installed, Premiere displays this error message: "This Motion Graphics template was created in After Effects. To use this template in Premiere, please install After Effects (trial or license) and retry."

Motion Graphics templates authored in After Effects require a trial or licensed installation of After Effects on the computer. Creative Cloud subscribers already have an After Effects license and can install it through the Creative Cloud application. With a Single-App license of Premiere, a paid After Effects license is not required; the After Effects trial version can be installed instead. After the trial period ends, the After Effects Motion Graphics template continues to work if the expired trial remains installed or if a paid After Effects version is installed. Leave the expired trial installed if you depend on that template workflow.
