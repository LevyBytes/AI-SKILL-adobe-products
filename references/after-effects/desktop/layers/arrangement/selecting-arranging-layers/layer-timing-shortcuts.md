# Use keyboard shortcuts to move, trim, or extend time for sets of layers

Moving or trimming several layers at once in the Timeline panel, you can hold their timing relative to one another by adding a modifier key. That way the chosen layers travel together as a group rather than every In or Out point collapsing onto a single time.

| Shortcut | Result |
| --- | --- |
| - Windows: [<br>- macOS: [ | Drops every selected layer's In point straight onto the CTI. |
| - Windows: ]<br>- macOS: ] | Drops every selected layer's Out point straight onto the CTI. |
| - Windows: Shift+[<br>- macOS: Shift+[ | Slides all selected layers until the earliest In point lands on the CT, carrying every layer along and holding their relative offsets. |
| - Windows: Shift+]<br>- macOS: Shift+] | Slides all selected layers until the latest Out point lands on the CTI, carrying every layer along and holding their relative offsets. |
| - Windows: Alt+[<br>- macOS: Option+[ | Trims or stretches every selected layer's In point straight onto the CTI. |
| - Windows: Alt+]<br>- macOS: Option+] | Trims or stretches every selected layer's Out point straight onto the CTI. |
| - Windows: Alt+Shift+[<br>- macOS: Option+Shift+[ | Trims or stretches all selected layers until the earliest In point lands on the CTI, holding their relative offsets. |
| - Windows: Alt+Shift+]<br>- macOS: Option+Shift+] | Trims or stretches all selected layers until the latest Out point lands on the CTI, holding their relative offsets. |
