# The expression UI

Working with expressions starts with one prerequisite: learning the interface. Walk through these steps to begin:

Build a comp, then choose Layer > New > Solid to drop in a solid layer.

Over in the Timeline panel, pick the layer and tap S on the keyboard to bring up the scale property. That's the launch point for your expressions journey.

Now build an expression. Inside the Timeline panel, Alt+Click (Windows) or Opt+Click (macOS) the stopwatch beside Position to attach an expression to that property. After Effects hands every property a default expression up front, and you can edit it afterward.

Watch how the interface shifts once the expression exists. To start, the Scale property's value (displayed as 100,100) turns from blue to red. That red signals an active expression on the value.

4. On the layer property, that red tint is the cue that an expression is live on the value.
   You'll spot a twirly that has shown up to the left of the stopwatch; click to open it. It reveals the expression itself.

- The first button resembles an equals sign ("=") and switches the expression on or off. While the expression is on, it shows blue.
- The second button flips on a view of the expression's value across time inside the Graph Editor.
- The third button, the one with the little spiral, is the pick whip, a helper for assembling expressions.
- The fourth button calls up the Expression Language menu, another aid for assembling expressions.

5. The Expression Language menu, found in the Timeline panel, speeds up dropping predefined methods and properties into expressions.
   Lastly, off to the right of those four buttons sits the default expression transform.position, ready to view. To swap it out, click the expression text in the timeline and the expression editor field goes live. It behaves like any text field — copy, paste, select, or drag the text. Hit enter for a new line, and you can drag the height larger. Click into it and type whatever expression you want, such as wiggle(3,50).

6. Here a Position expression shifts the position three times per second, straying up to 50 pixels in any direction.
