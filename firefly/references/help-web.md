## Adobe Firefly FAQ

Find answers to common questions about working with Adobe Firefly.

### About Adobe Firefly

Adobe Firefly is a standalone web app at firefly.adobe.com. It uses generative AI to give people additional ways to brainstorm, make content, and communicate, while also improving creative workflows. Beyond the Firefly website, Adobe provides the wider Firefly family of creative generative AI models, plus Firefly-powered features in Adobe flagship apps and Adobe Stock.

Firefly extends the technology Adobe has built over 40 years, with the idea that people should be able to bring imagined concepts into the world accurately.

Adobe works to keep Firefly smooth to use, but an account may be restricted when activity does not comply with guidelines, policies, or terms. Firefly feature use requires that you:

- Use a stable Internet connection.
- Use an Adobe ID.
- Have an active entitlement to Adobe Genuine software that approves Firefly-powered feature use within Creative Cloud apps.
- Comply with Adobe's generative AI user guidelines.
- Be located where Adobe Firefly is available. Adobe Firefly is not available in Russia, Belarus, and China. Adobe services and products are also unavailable in Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea, and the territories referred to as the Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics.

If questions remain and you believe your usage complies with those guidelines, contact us.

Firefly's accessibility approach follows the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, abbreviated WCAG, at 2.1 Level A and Level AA. Firefly supports accessibility features including keyboard navigation, screen reader use, color contrast, and forms.

- Keyboard: Firefly supports Tab for moving between user interface controls, Space or Enter for activating buttons and links, arrow keys for moving between options, and Space for selecting options.
- Operating system accessibility: Firefly supplies operating-system user interface information so assistive technologies can interact with it, including VoiceOver on macOS and NVDA on Windows.
- Color contrast: Firefly's interface targets WCAG 2.1 AA color contrast guidelines.
- Forms: Forms in Firefly do not have error states.

Images displayed in Firefly inspiration streams are pre-cached samples and are not unique per user. Viewing samples does not spend generative credits, but credits are used if you perform an action that generates more images, such as selecting Refresh.

### Using Firefly

For features that do not carry the beta label, Firefly-generated outputs may be used in commercial projects. For beta features, Firefly-generated outputs may also be used commercially unless the product explicitly says otherwise.

Results vary with the prompt, because prompts strongly guide the AI's behavior and affect response quality and relevance. Write descriptive prompts to produce striking, vivid images and video; if the output is not what you want, revise the prompt to move closer to your goal.

When your plan includes Firefly entitlement, it cannot be disabled, but you can simply avoid Firefly-powered workflows in your apps or choose not to sign in to the Firefly website.

Submitting work to the Firefly community means you allow Adobe to use the generated image and prompt in marketing materials and on inspiration and gallery pages; attribution may include your Adobe profile photo and name. This also lets others create from the same prompt. Adobe's design team reviews and curates contributions. Do not submit community content from Business profiles, including end users of Enterprise, Teams, or Education accounts.

### Data and content usage

Current Firefly generative AI models were trained with licensed content, including Adobe Stock, together with public domain material. As Firefly develops, Adobe is looking into ways creators could train the machine learning model with their own assets, letting them generate content aligned to their individual style, branding, and design language without influence from other creators' content. Adobe will keep listening to and working with the creative community as Firefly training models evolve.

No. Adobe does not train on Creative Cloud subscribers' personal content. For Adobe Stock contributors, their content is included in Firefly's training dataset under Stock Contributor license agreements.

To support Adobe's goal of making Firefly commercially safe, the first commercial Firefly model is trained on Adobe Stock images, openly licensed content, and public domain content whose copyright has expired.

Adobe is also a founding collaborator of the Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI), and through that work helps define industry practice for responsible generative AI. The CAI brings together media and technology companies, NGOs, academics, and others to encourage adoption of an open industry standard for content authenticity and provenance.

This work is paired with the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA), which created an open technical standard that helps publishers, creators, and consumers understand where different media types came from. It also supports adding a Content Credential that lets a user indicate generative AI was used. Learn more about content credentials.

Generative AI law is changing quickly in jurisdictions worldwide. For copyright questions, contact a local legal service provider who understands the current law. For more discussion, see the Copyright Alliance article Protecting Creators in the Age of Generative AI.

Adobe is committed to opposing misinformation, including through participation in the Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI), a global coalition that brings transparency to digital assets with metadata called Content Credentials. Content Credentials act like a digital "nutrition label" where creators can add tamper-evident details such as creation date, tools used, and edits made. Adobe automatically applies Content Credentials for some export types that use Firefly outputs, showing that an AI tool was used. This context helps viewers make a more informed judgment about whether to trust the asset.

## Generative credits overview

### Understand generative credits and how they function.

Generative AI refers to artificial intelligence that can turn simple text prompts into creative output. In Adobe apps, Adobe Firefly generative AI technology is intended for play, experimentation, dreaming, and making extraordinary work.

Early coverage centered on images, text effects, and vectors:

- Adobe Photoshop: Generative Fill and Generative Expand
- Adobe Firefly: Generate Image
- Adobe Illustrator: Generative Recolor
- Adobe Express: Generate Text Effects
- and more

Firefly-powered generative AI now also reaches 3D, animation, and video through Adobe Firefly features such as Generate Video and Translate and Lip Sync.

Plans for Creative Cloud provide a monthly generative credit allocation. Those credits cover generative AI content creation across features in Creative Cloud, Adobe Express, and Adobe Substance 3D applications. Credit allotments differ by plan, and usage varies with both the generative AI feature selected and the type of subscription.

See details for Generative credits and the amounts included with specific plans.

You can also buy credits through the Adobe Firefly Standard and Firefly Pro plans.

## Content Credentials overview

### See how Content Credentials support Adobe's generative AI transparency approach and what metadata appears when they are added automatically.

### Content Credentials in Adobe Firefly

Content Credentials are tamper-evident, industry-standard metadata that supplies context about a file's creation or edits and the parties involved. The metadata can carry many kinds of details; in Adobe Firefly, its role is to make generative AI use more transparent.

To support transparency for generative AI, Adobe automatically adds Content Credentials to assets whose pixels are 100% generated with Adobe Firefly, including Text to Image.

### What information is included?

Content Credentials that Adobe Firefly applies automatically to generated assets always contain these non-personal details:

- Output thumbnail: a Firefly website visual preview for assets made with Text to image.
- Issuer: Adobe Inc., which issues the Content Credential.
- Date: the issue date for the Content Credential.
- App or device used: the Adobe software application or hardware device that generated the asset.
- AI tool used: the specific Adobe generative AI tool involved.
- Actions: broad creation, editing, and processing activity used to make the asset. For assets generated with Adobe Firefly features, the entries are limited to Created or Other edits.

If you have not chosen to add more information through Content Credentials in Photoshop, Lightroom, or Content Authenticity, nothing else is added.

### Where are Content Credentials stored?

Assets generated with Adobe Firefly features carry Content Credentials on their files, and a copy may also be stored in Adobe's public Content Credentials cloud.

Keeping a copy in Adobe's Content Credentials cloud improves durability and recovery through the Inspect tool on Adobe Content Authenticity. When a selected asset has associated Content Credentials, Inspect can display them.

Note

Inspect is for recovering Content Credentials, not for gallery browsing or search.

### What happens to my unlimited generations after a year?

Once the first year ends, users go back to credit consumption. Required credits differ by model and resolution, and the account picker always shows the remaining credit balance.

### What defines a year?

Your promotional year starts on the date your eligible plan begins and continues for the next 12 consecutive months. For example, a subscription started on May 21, 2026 keeps promotional benefits available through May 20, 2027.

### Where can I access my unlimited generations special offer?

Access is limited to firefly.adobe.com and the Firefly mobile app. Other Adobe products and desktop applications are outside the offer.

### Could the models included change during the offer?

Models and resolutions covered by the unlimited generation promotion can change. Adobe keeps adjusting the mix of models and may add or remove options because of availability, performance, or other factors. When a partner model stops being available through Firefly, it also drops out of the promotion.

### Could the way "unlimited" works change during the offer?

Yes. Adobe may change how unlimited generations operate while improving Firefly by adding product functionality, usage management features, or service safeguards, including adjusted processing speeds or queueing when demand is high. These measures are intended to preserve a high-quality experience for customers. Adobe reserves the right to modify, limit, or discontinue any part of the promotion at any time.

### Are all models and resolutions included in unlimited?

No. The select models and resolutions covered by unlimited generations differ by plan. Use the plan-by-plan breakdown below to see every included model and resolution.
