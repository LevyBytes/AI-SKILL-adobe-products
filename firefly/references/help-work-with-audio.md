## Translate audio

### Translate audio clips into multiple languages while keeping the original voice character.

Firefly Translate audio converts spoken material into other languages. It can be used for audio such as podcasts or speeches, helping make content available to wider audiences for business, education, or personal needs.

Before you begin:

Choose an audio file in .mp3, aac, or .wav format. It must be at least five seconds and no longer than 10 minutes, with at least five seconds of continuous audible speech and little reverb or background noise.

In Firefly, open the Audio section and select Translate audio.

Select Browse files and upload the file you want to translate.

After a successful upload, Source language (auto-detected) under Translate audio displays the detected language.

Tip

Use Source language (auto-detected) if you need to change the source language manually.

Choose one or more target languages from the Translate into dropdown menu.

Use Translate into to pick several languages for translating the file.

The Translate into dropdown menu supports selecting multiple target languages for the file.

Tip

The generated transcript cannot be edited. If transcript correction is needed before translation, Adobe Translate and Lipsync APIs may be the better workflow; ask your CSM for details.

Select Generate.

Note

Before starting translation with Generate, check the balance indicator to see your plan's available credit balance.

When translation finishes, use the Language dropdown menu to choose a translated language and inspect the result.

Use Language to review translated versions before downloading.

The Language dropdown menu lets you check each translated version before download.

Select Download to get the translated version as a .wav file.

Content Credentials are attached to the generated dubbed file so viewers can tell that generative AI was part of the creative process. The file also appears in Your media and remains downloadable for seven days; after that, it is permanently deleted.

## Frequently asked questions about Translate audio and Translate video

### Answers to common Translate video and Translate audio questions.

### 1. How do Content Credentials appear in Translate video and Translate audio output?

Content Credentials are attached to content made with Translate video and Translate audio so viewers can identify that generative AI contributed to the creative process.

For Translate video and Translate audio output, Content Credentials indicate that AI generated speaker voices, translated languages, and adjusted new lip movements.

### 2. Why is transcript editing unavailable before audio translation in the web app?

The website starts with a simple customer workflow for audio translation. Transcript editing is available through the API.

### 3. How can I report output that seems wrong, offensive, or harmful?

Use the Report tool to send negative or inaccurate results.

### 4. When are more Translate video and Translate audio languages coming, and how can I request mine?

Additional languages are in progress, but no dates are available yet. If your language is not supported, use Submit an idea from the upper-right dropdown menu on the Adobe Firefly website, or post on Adobe Community Forum.

### 5. Why are Translate video and Translate audio unavailable in some locations?

Access to Adobe Firefly is limited to countries where Adobe services are offered and may also be subject to other restrictions.

### 6. Which recordings work best with Translate video and Translate audio?

Use a video or audio recording that contains at least five seconds of spoken voice.

Best results come from clear audio with audible speech and little reverb or background noise.

At present, single-speaker recordings are supported; frequent turns between multiple speakers can produce poor results.

The recording should use one spoken language from beginning to end.

### 7. Which video and audio file specifications are supported?

Files for translation and/or lip sync must be at least five seconds and up to 10 minutes long, and the file needs at least five seconds of uninterrupted audio.

For video files:

Input video support: Up to 4K resolution

Containers: .mp4 and .mov

For audio files:

Containers: .mp3, aac, and .wav

### 8. Which output formats do Translate video and Translate audio create?

Generated video output uses .mp4, while generated audio output uses .wav.

### 9. How can I retrieve processed media after leaving the website?

If you exit while processing is still running, return within 7 days to view the processed media. Reopening Translate video and Translate audio shows the file as a page thumbnail.

## Writing effective text prompts for sound effects generation

### Write stronger text prompts for generating sound effects with Text to sound effects and Voice to sound effects.

Text to sound effects and Voice to sound effects create sound-effect audio clips from written prompts and recorded audio guidance. When a text prompt is paired with a voice performance, the generated sound effect can follow the prompt description while matching the timing and energy in the voice performance. For atmospheric soundscapes, impactful effects, or ambient background noise that supports visual storytelling, clear prompt writing helps these features work to their full potential.

Describe the sounds you want in language that is clear, concise, and direct. Focus on the audible qualities of the sound itself.

Use direct adjectives for the sound effect's qualities and verbs that express how the sound behaves or acts. Those choices guide the sound's detailed characteristics.

Comma-separated keywords or descriptions can quickly capture several traits of the target sound. This format also supports prompt-writing best practices while keeping the description short enough to communicate complex sound effects clearly.

Generate Sound Effects is built to create one sound at a time for stronger quality and control. To build soundscapes with multiple sounds, generate each sound on its own and layer the results with multiple audio tracks. Post-generation edit options can then fine-tune timing and volume, and each sound can be downloaded for project use or further editing.

For ambient soundscapes, broad or general descriptions often work better than highly specific prompts. Prompts with too much detail may produce outputs that feel less organic or too literal.

## Remove noise and improve voice quality

### Use Enhance Speech to turn recordings into studio-quality audio.

Enhance Speech improves recordings by reducing background noise, balancing volume, and increasing clarity. The processed audio sounds cleaner and sharper, similar to a recording made in a studio or professional booth.

Note

Premium users can use bulk upload and video upload. See the Enhance Speech plan details for more information.

On the Firefly homepage, choose Generate in the left panel; on the Audio tab, select Enhance Speech.

On Enhance Speech, upload files by dragging and dropping video or audio files, or by selecting Choose files.

Select one uploaded file to start enhancing its speech.

Select one uploaded file to start enhancing its speech.

Use the sliders below the preview area to adjust speech and background levels separately:

- Speech: changes how much enhancement is applied to isolated speech so it becomes clearer and more prominent. Moving the slider left reduces speech enhancement and makes speech less prominent; moving it right increases enhancement and makes speech clearer and more distinct.
- Background: changes the loudness of isolated background noise, making it quieter or louder as needed. Moving the slider left lowers background noise volume, while moving it right raises it.
