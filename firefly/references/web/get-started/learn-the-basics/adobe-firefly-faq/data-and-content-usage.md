# Data and content usage

Current Firefly generative AI models were trained with licensed content, including Adobe Stock, together with public domain material. As Firefly develops, Adobe is looking into ways creators could train the machine learning model with their own assets, letting them generate content aligned to their individual style, branding, and design language without influence from other creators' content. Adobe will keep listening to and working with the creative community as Firefly training models evolve.

No. Adobe does not train on Creative Cloud subscribers' personal content. For Adobe Stock contributors, their content is included in Firefly's training dataset under Stock Contributor license agreements.

To support Adobe's goal of making Firefly commercially safe, the first commercial Firefly model is trained on Adobe Stock images, openly licensed content, and public domain content whose copyright has expired.

Adobe is also a founding collaborator of the Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI), and through that work helps define industry practice for responsible generative AI. The CAI brings together media and technology companies, NGOs, academics, and others to encourage adoption of an open industry standard for content authenticity and provenance.

This work is paired with the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA), which created an open technical standard that helps publishers, creators, and consumers understand where different media types came from. It also supports adding a Content Credential that lets a user indicate generative AI was used. Learn more about content credentials.

Generative AI law is changing quickly in jurisdictions worldwide. For copyright questions, contact a local legal service provider who understands the current law. For more discussion, see the Copyright Alliance article Protecting Creators in the Age of Generative AI.

Adobe is committed to opposing misinformation, including through participation in the Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI), a global coalition that brings transparency to digital assets with metadata called Content Credentials. Content Credentials act like a digital "nutrition label" where creators can add tamper-evident details such as creation date, tools used, and edits made. Adobe automatically applies Content Credentials for some export types that use Firefly outputs, showing that an AI tool was used. This context helps viewers make a more informed judgment about whether to trust the asset.
