# Generate sound effects using your voice as a guide

Use Voice to sound effects to generate sound effects guided by your recorded voice.

Voice to sound effects lets a voice recording steer sound-effect generation. The recording supplies timing on the timeline and loudness cues; paired with a text prompt, Firefly uses that timing and energy to create sound effects that follow the recording's dynamics.

Combine a recorded voice guide with a text prompt so Firefly can align the sound effect with the voice's timing and energy.

Voice to sound effects is useful when sound effects must hit exact moments in the timeline. It helps avoid the extra work caused by clips that are too long or too short for the actions or events in the video or audio timeline.

Your voice recording serves only as a timing and energy guide for the generated sound effect. The feature does not create speech or sung lyrics.

From the Adobe Firefly homepage, open the Audio module and choose Voice to sound effects.

On Generate Sound Effects, choose Upload media to add the video or audio file that will be used while composing sound effects.

Use Upload media to add a file in .mp4, .mp3, .mov, .wav, or .aac format.

Place the playhead at the timeline location where the sound effect should be inserted.

Move along the timeline and set the playhead precisely where the sound effect belongs.

In Voice performance, either upload an audio file from your device or choose Record voice to begin recording.

Note

Voice recording is limited to 30 seconds or to the video duration, whichever is shorter, and must stay inside the allowed limit.

After the three-second countdown finishes, the Recording indicator turns on and recording can begin.

Record your voice as an approximate guide for how the finished generated variations should sound.

In Voice recording, use the play button to hear the recording.

The prompt field shows that a voice recording has been added. Enter a text prompt for the sound effect that should be combined with the recording to generate variations.

For a lion roar matched to your video, for example, enter the text prompt "lion roaring" and record yourself roaring in sync with your video.

Choose Generate, then select one generated sound effect variation to place it at the playhead's position on the timeline.

To swap out a timeline sound effect, select the existing sound effect first, then choose a replacement from the generated variations.

The generated clip can be edited with options such as mute and volume adjustment. Download, rename, and delete are also available.
