## Your stuff overview

Explore the Your stuff menu in Adobe Firefly to reach files, projects, and creative assets from one centralized place.

Your stuff is in the left panel and acts as a central Firefly area for organizing, opening, and managing the content you create.

This area keeps the workflow structured by sorting content into logical sections, making assets easier to find and use no matter their type or creation date.

### Accessing Your stuff

Choose Your stuff in the left panel to reach personal files, projects, shared files, and generation history. Its sections separate content types so each area is easier to manage:

Your stuff gives quick access to files, history, favorites, and related areas.

Use Your stuff to reach files, history, favorites, and more.

### Your files

Your files contains individual Adobe files, including images, videos, other assets opened or created through Adobe Home (including Express, Acrobat, and other Adobe apps), plus exported files from Firefly projects.

### Shared with you

Shared with you lists files and projects that other people have shared with you. It supports these tasks:

- Open files shared by teammates and collaborators.
- See projects where you have been invited to collaborate.
- Leave feedback on shared content.
- Download shared assets or make copies when permissions allow.

### Projects

Projects helps group related files for better organization. It can collect multiple assets from different Adobe apps into one central space so creative work, permissions, and sharing are easier to manage. Projects supports these actions:

- Sort content by client, campaign, or purpose, and share it with groups instead of separate individuals.
- Share full collections with collaborators at once without moving or duplicating files.

### Favorites

Favorites gives fast access to generations you have marked as favorites. Mark generations as favorites when you want to return to assets quickly or reuse them later.

### Generation history

Generation history helps locate previously created audio, video, or image generations so generative work can be tracked. For details, see how to view your generation history.

### Style kits

In Style kits, open your style kits and see Style Kits that others shared with you or that you created. You can also choose Start generating images to begin image generation with Text to image.

### Deleted

Deleted works as a recycle bin for Firefly content, temporarily storing recently deleted files and projects. Accidentally removed content can be recovered during the typical limited retention period before permanent deletion.

## View generation history

Go to the Generation history tab to find previous Firefly generations.

Firefly stores generated media - images, audio, and video - in the Generation history tab under Your stuff. Items in this area use allocated Adobe Cloud storage.

### Access your generation history

On the Firefly homepage, choose Your Stuff from the left panel.

Open the Generation history tab.

Generation history lists the content you have generated previously.

That tab is where previous generated content is collected.

Use the dropdown menu to filter the view to images, videos, audio, or all generated content.

Hover over any image, audio, or video in generation history.

Open the context menu by right-clicking or choosing the three dots.

Choose from these options:

- Open: opens the selected generation in the editor view for editing.
- Favorite: adds the item to Favorites for quick access.
- Download: saves the selected generation output to your local device.
- Add to: puts the generation in a specific project, or saves it to a folder in Your files.
- Open in boards: sends the selected generation to a new or existing Firefly board.
- Share public link: makes a public link for sharing the generation with anyone.
- Delete permanently: removes the item from generation history.

Video generations also include Open in video editor (beta).

For video generations, Open in video editor (beta) is also shown.

For images, additional options appear:

- Open in Photoshop web: creates a new Photoshop on the web file from the selected image.
- Open in Adobe Express: creates a new Adobe Express file from the selected image.

Note

Generation history is stored in Adobe Cloud storage; like other cloud-saved files, it uses your allocated storage space.

## Save generations to Favorites

Keep preferred generated images, videos, and audios in Adobe Firefly as Favorites so the content you like most stays easy to find and manage.

In Firefly, any content generation can be marked as a favorite. After you favorite an item, Firefly places it in the dedicated Favorites section under Your stuff, which makes retrieval simpler. This helps when you have produced several variations and need a reliable way to track the options you prefer.

### Save content to Favorites

Open Your stuff > Generation history, then find the generation that should become a favorite. You can also choose one of the generation results directly.

Hover over the content and choose the star icon, or choose the three dots to display the menu.

Save the file as a Favorite for quick access later.

Favorites keeps the file easier to locate again.

In the menu that appears, choose Favorite.

Allow the confirmation message to appear, confirming that the content has been saved to favorites.

Tip

To open saved favorites, use Your stuff > Favorites.

Note

Images produced with Custom models and Object composites are not automatically saved in the Generation history tab. Adobe plans to add this enterprise-feature capability in a future release.

## Organize your Firefly generations

### Add copies of items from the Generation history tab to Projects or Your files for easier asset management.

You can keep Firefly generations together with assets from other Adobe apps, including Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign. With those generations in the same places as existing projects and personal files, you can organize, share, and collaborate on generative content more easily.

Adding a generation to Projects or Your files creates a copy. That copy can be organized by project while the full generation history remains available, so the creative process is still traceable.

### Add generations to Projects or Your files

Open Your stuff > Generation history.

Choose one or more generations to organize.

In the toolbar at the screen's lower edge, choose Add to....

Choose several assets and send them to one shared space.

Multiple selected assets can be added to the same destination space.

In the location picker, choose the destination: for personal storage, pick a folder in Your files; for team collaboration, pick a Project.

Choose the location, then select Add so your generations are copied into that space.

After organizing generations elsewhere, you can manually delete the originals from the Generation history tab if you no longer want them there. Only Generation history is affected; copies added to other spaces stay available. Learn how projects can be used to organize content.

## Share a public link to Firefly generation

Use these steps to make shareable links for Firefly-generated content.

Public links let you send Firefly generations to collaborators, clients, team members, or anyone else. Anyone who has the URL can view the Firefly generation and its related details, including prompts and reference materials.

Recipients need to sign in with an Adobe account before viewing the shared generation. A free Adobe account is enough to access shared content; a paid subscription is not required.

### Create and share a public link

Open the Adobe Firefly homepage and choose Your stuff.

Choose Generation history to display the generations you have saved.

Right-click the generation to share, or choose the three-dots control to open the context menu.

Choose Share public link.

Use Share public link to copy the link to the clipboard automatically.

The public-link command places the copied link on the clipboard.

Wait until the confirmation message appears before sharing the copied URL.

Note

After a generation is shared publicly, its link continues to work until the original asset is deleted. Delete the original generation from your history to revoke access through that public link.

## Access your generation history in other Adobe apps

Use Firefly generations inside other Adobe products, including Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign.

Firefly generation history connects with other Adobe apps, so generative content can be reused across Adobe products without interrupting the creative workflow. Through Adobe cloud storage, app-compatible generations are available in Adobe Photoshop (web and desktop), Adobe Illustrator, and Adobe InDesign.

An active Creative Cloud subscription is required, with access to Firefly and to the destination apps or web apps. Depending on the app and workflow, there are two ways to reach Firefly generations.

### Place generations using the file picker

Inside the relevant Adobe app, choose the option that matches that app:

- Photoshop desktop: File > Place Embedded > Open cloud documents
- Illustrator: File > Place > Open cloud documents
- InDesign: File > Place > Open Cloud Document
- Photoshop web: Hamburger menu > Import

Tip

In Photoshop web, another route is Add image > Upload an image > Generation history from the toolbar, which opens Firefly generation history.

Browse the folders listed there to find the Firefly generation.

Note

The Generation history tab shows only generations that work with the app you are currently using, which reduces time spent searching for usable assets.

Choose the generation to use, then confirm the selection so it is added to the document.

### Create documents from Generation history

In Photoshop (desktop or web) or Illustrator, find the Generation history tab and select it.

All compatible generations already appear in the Generation history or Firefly history tab.

All compatible generations already appear in the Generation history or Firefly history tab.

Review the compatible generations shown in the tab and select one.

A new document is then created automatically, using the selected generation as its main content.
