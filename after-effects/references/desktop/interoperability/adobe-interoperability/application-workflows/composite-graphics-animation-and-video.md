# Composite graphics, animation, and video

Both Animate and After Effects pack plenty of tools for compositing video and graphics in sophisticated ways. The right choice between them comes down to what you prefer and what kind of finished output you're after.

| After Effects | Animate |
| --- | --- |
| Geared toward video and film production, After Effects supplies a broad palette of visual effects and typically turns out video files as its end product. | Of the two, Animate leans toward the web thanks to its compact final file size. It also supports controlling animation at run time. |
| Composite over imported video in After Effects, on the other hand, and the exported video file genuinely bakes in the composited graphics and effects. | Compositing leaves the actual video content untouched — it changes only how the video looks while it plays back in Flash Player. |
| Every drawing and painting in After Effects lands on layers kept apart from any imported video, so the work is always non-destructive. | Animate offers drawing in both destructive and non-destructive modes. |
| Same as Adobe Animate, though After Effects ships a deeper set of effects. | Ccreate original artwork plus animation, leaning on a timeline and exposing scripting hooks so animation can be driven programmatically. |
| Lets you set graphics on their own layers for compositing, layers you can toggle on and off whenever you need. Both programs also let you apply effects to whatever sits on an individual layer. | Same as After Effects. |
