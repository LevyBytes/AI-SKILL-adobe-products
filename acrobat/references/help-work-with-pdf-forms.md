## PDF forms overview

### Get to know the main capabilities and the kinds of PDF forms that Adobe Acrobat lets you build and handle.

With a PDF form, you type your information straight into the fields set aside for it. Where an ordinary PDF mostly exists to be read, a PDF form gathers data by way of interactive parts—text fields, checkboxes, radio buttons, and dropdown menus among them. You can get to such forms across the web or through email.

### Types of PDF forms

PDF forms come in two principal varieties:

- Interactive forms: built with fields you click into and complete on screen. Because those fields are set up ahead of time and easy to spot, entering data is a simple matter.
- Flat forms: scanned paper documents or PDFs that carry no interactive fields for you to type the information into.

### Key features of PDF forms

To build, complete, sign, and distribute PDF forms electronically, turn to the Fill & Sign tool in Acrobat. What it offers for this work breaks down as:

- Field recognition: spots the static form fields sitting in scanned documents and turns them, on its own, into fillable interactive text fields.
- Dynamic fields: drop in interactive buttons and e-signature fields, plus other form elements, to gather further information.
- Distribution and response tracking: hand out PDF forms by email or direct link, keep an eye on responses, and nudge recipients with reminders.
- Interactive functionality: reach for JavaScript to make PDF forms more interactive—think involved calculations, data validations, and buttons wired to specific actions.

Through PDF form preferences, you tailor the way you handle and work with PDF forms. Form field behavior, appearance, and auto-complete functions all fall under these settings. Tuning the preferences can make working with PDF forms both more efficient and more consistent.

## Convert documents to PDF forms

### See how Adobe Acrobat turns your scans and documents into forms people can fill in.

When you convert scanned paper forms or digital files, Acrobat spots the static form fields on its own and turns them into fillable text fields. Moving text fields, inserting buttons, adding or deleting form fields, and dragging and dropping are all within reach. On top of that, you can switch on fields for electronic signatures.

Select All tools > Prepare a form.

Pick whichever of these options suits the situation:

- Select a file: takes a document you already have and makes it a PDF form.
- Scan a document: captures a paper form by scanner and renders it as a PDF form.

Planning to collect signatures from others? Turn on This document requires signatures.

Select Create form, which opens the form ready for editing.

Look over the fields that came out of the conversion. Delete, resize, or rearrange fields as needed.

To bring the existing form fields into view, choose the visible fields over in the Fields pane.

To bring the existing form fields into view, choose the visible fields over in the Fields pane.

If you'd like those fields highlighted on the form, open the Options menu in the Fields pane and choose Highlight existing fields.

When you want extra text fields or form components, pick the options you need from ADD FORM COMPONENTS under Prepare a form pane.

To rework text or an image already on the page, choose Edit from the Global bar, then click the field or image in question.

For a preview, choose Preview over in the Prepare a form pane.

Save your fillable form.

## Create PDF forms from scratch

### See how to build a fillable PDF form in Adobe Acrobat beginning from an empty page.

Adobe Acrobat lets you build a fillable form entirely from scratch. Begin with a blank PDF, put in the text and labels you need, drop in form components, and then fine-tune how your form fields are laid out and formatted.

Select Menu (Windows) or File (macOS) > Prepare a form.

When the Prepare a form window comes up, choose Start from blank page.

If signatures from others will be required, switch on This document requires signatures.

Select Create form, and a blank PDF form comes up ready for editing.

For text, choose Text under ADD CONTENT in the Prepare a form pane, click where you want it on the page, and type what you need.

Need additional form fields? From ADD FORM COMPONENTS in the left pane, grab whichever component fits—say an Image Field, a Checkbox, or a Radio button.

To add components, select the option from the ADD FORM COMPONENTS pane.

To add components, select the option from the ADD FORM COMPONENTS pane.

To delete a form field, right-click the field and select Delete.

Select Preview to see how your form will appear to the recipients and save the form.

## Align form fields

### See how lining up form fields in Adobe Acrobat keeps your PDF forms easy to work with.

Select All tools > Prepare a form.

Using Shift + Click, pick out the two or more fields you intend to align.

Head to the Prepare a form pane and, beneath ALIGN, choose from these:

- Left align: lines the fields up against the left edge of the leftmost one you selected.
- Right align: lines the fields up against the right edge of the rightmost one you selected.
- Top align: lines the fields up against the top edge of the highest one you selected.
- Bottom align: lines the fields up against the bottom edge of the lowest one you selected.
- Align horizontal center: sets the fields on a shared horizontal center point.
- Align vertical center: sets the fields on a shared vertical center point.

To align the form fields, select the appropriate option from the ALIGN and CENTER sections.

To align the form fields, select the appropriate option from the ALIGN and CENTER sections.

For centering fields on the page, reach for any of these options under CENTER:

- Center horizontally: places the fields at the page's horizontal midpoint.
- Center vertically: places the fields at the page's vertical midpoint.
- Center both: sets the fields at the page's center on both axes at once.

To fine-tune the gaps between aligned fields, pick any of the following under DISTRIBUTE:

- Distribute Vertically: gives the fields equal spacing running top to bottom.
- Distribute Horizontally: gives the fields equal spacing running left to right.

Choose Preview to confirm the fields ended up properly aligned.

## Copy form fields

### See how Adobe Acrobat lets you duplicate form fields inside a PDF form.

Copy a form field and you no longer have to set properties such as size, font, and fill order over and over for every field. Because the duplicates carry over all the original properties, the form keeps a consistent look and feel throughout. Copying and pasting lets you spin up many fields sharing the same attributes in short order, which speeds the form creation workflow.

### Copy a single form field

Select All tools > Prepare a form.

Find the field you mean to duplicate, right-click it, and choose Copy.

Right-click somewhere on the page and choose Paste; the duplicate lands at the page's center.

Grab the duplicate and drag it wherever you want it.

Tip

While dragging, hold down Shift to lock the movement to either vertical or horizontal.

If you'd like the copied field to fill in automatically with whatever was entered in the original, leave the field name as it is.

When the form field is meant for unique information, rename the field.

Find the field you'd like to duplicate, right-click it, and pick Create Multiple Copies.

Nudge the duplicates into place with the Up, Down, Left, and Right buttons.

Confirm with OK to generate the copies.

## Move form fields

### Reposition fields on a PDF form in Adobe Acrobat and reshape its layout with ease.

Start by opening a fillable PDF form.

Open All tools > Prepare a form.

Pick the field — or several fields — that you intend to relocate.

Use whichever of these fits the move you need:

- Reposition fields by dragging your selection wherever you want it to sit.
- Hold Shift as you drag if the motion should stay purely horizontal or vertical.
- For an exact spot, right-click the field, pick Properties, open the Position tab, and key in the Position values.
- For fine horizontal or vertical adjustments, tap the arrow keys to inch the selected field along.
- To recenter a field on the page, right-click it and pick Cut, go to where it belongs, then pick Paste.

## Resize form fields

### Resize the fields on a PDF form, the Adobe Acrobat way.

### Resize a single form field

Start by opening a fillable PDF form.

Open All tools > Prepare a form.

Pick the field whose size you want to change.

Dragging any border handle resizes it by hand.

For precise measurements, right-click the field and pick Properties, then edit the Width and Height values in the Position tab.

### Set form fields to the same size

Open All tools > Prepare a form.

Hold Ctrl - click (Windows) or Command - click (macOS) to pick out the fields you'd like to resize.

Right-click your selection and pick Set Fields to Same Size.

Pick whichever of these suits your needs:

- Height: brings every selected field to a matching height.
- Width: brings every selected field to a matching width.
- Both: matches every selected field on both height and width.

## Check if a PDF form is fillable

### Spot a PDF's fillable fields with Adobe Acrobat's Pan tool.

Open your form in Acrobat and grab the Pan tool from the Quick action toolbar.

Sweep the Pan tool over the fields in the PDF. Where the form carries fillable fields, the Pan tool swaps to one of these icons:

- I-beam: lets you type text into a text field.
- Pointing hand: lets you pick an option or tick a box.

Note

On Acrobat 3D or Acrobat Professional, confirming that a form is fillable is as simple as picking the Form tool, which lights up every form field.

## Fill forms automatically

### Switch on Adobe Acrobat's Auto-Complete feature, tweak how it works, and clear stored entries out of form fields.

As you type into an interactive form field, Acrobat's Auto-Complete feature remembers what you entered and offers it back as a suggestion or fills it in for you. It ships switched off, though flipping it on takes only a moment.

### Enable Auto-Complete

Go to Menu > Preferences (Windows) or Acrobat > Preferences (macOS).

Under Categories, pick Forms.

Open the Auto-Complete drop-down menu and pick Basic or Advanced.

- Basic: type a single character into a field and a drop-down lists just the likeliest matches.
- Advanced: tab into a field and, where likely matches exist, they appear in a list; a very likely one drops straight into the field.

Turn on Remember numerical data (e.g., telephone number) when you want Auto-Complete to retain the numbers you put into forms.

Confirm with OK.

### Delete Auto-Complete Entries

Go to Menu > Preferences (Windows) or Acrobat > Preferences (macOS).

Under Categories, pick Forms.

Under Auto-Complete, pick Edit Entry List.

In the Auto-Complete Entry List dialog box that opens, do one of these:

- Remove all entries: pick Remove All.
- Remove specific entries: highlight the ones you want gone, then pick Remove.

Pick Yes to confirm.

Pick Close.
