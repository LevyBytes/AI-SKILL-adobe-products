# Renumber pages in PDFs

How to revise or tailor the page numbers in your PDF so they line up with the document's structure and reading flow gets easier.

What shows up in thumbnails and the navigation toolbar won't always agree with the numbers printed on the page. By default the numbering runs in order from 1, yet PDFs frequently open with front matter — think a cover page or a table of contents.

So the shown numbers track your document's structure, give introductory pages Roman numerals, body pages plain numbers, and tack on prefixes for chapter-based schemes. As an illustration, 1-1, 1-2 for Chapter 1 alongside 2-1, 2-2 for Chapter 2.

Pick All tools > Organize pages > Page labels.

Once the Page labels dialog box appears, name a page range.

Pick one of these:

- Begin new section: Kicks off a fresh numbering run. Pick a style, type the starting number, and tack on a prefix if you like.
- Extend numbering used in preceding section to selected pages: Carries the numbering forward from earlier pages and leaves the sequence unbroken.

Tailor PDF page numbering by kicking off a fresh sequence, stretching one further, or carrying it on from earlier pages.

Pick OK to put it into effect.

Note

All the Page labels tool touches are the labels shown in thumbnails and page navigation — it won't print page numbers onto the page. When you want numbers visible on the document itself, turn to the Header and footer tool.
