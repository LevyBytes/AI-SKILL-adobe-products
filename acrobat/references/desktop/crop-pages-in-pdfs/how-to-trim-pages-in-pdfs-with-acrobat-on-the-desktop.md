# How to trim pages in PDFs with Acrobat on the desktop.

Trimming lets you cut away unwanted margins or zero in on particular material inside a PDF page. Everyone gets the basic trimming choices in Acrobat, whereas the advanced ones — tweaking TrimBox or BleedBox among them — show up only in Acrobat Pro.

Pick Edit from the top toolbar.

Pick Crop page over in the left pane.

Pull a rectangle across the area you mean to keep. Fine-tune that cropping rectangle by pulling its corner handles.

Double-click within the cropping rectangle.

Adjust the page box settings, page size, page range, and margins over in the Set Page Boxes dialog.

Within the Set Page Boxes dialog box, tweak whichever of these settings you need:

- Show All Boxes: Each box gets its own outline color — black for CropBox, red for ArtBox, green for TrimBox, blue for BleedBox. Where boxes overlap, a single line shows.

  - CropBox: the region that prints or stays visible.
  - ArtBox (Acrobat Pro): the principal content zone, white space and all.
  - TrimBox (Acrobat Pro): how big the page ends up once trimmed.
  - BleedBox (Acrobat Pro): the stretch past the trim meant for professional printing.

- Constrain Proportions: Holds every margin equal.
- Remove White Margins: Pares white space down to the artwork.
- Set To Zero: Clears all margins back to nothing.
- Revert To Selection: Goes back to the crop area you picked before.

Want these settings on further pages? Name the page range or pick All beneath Page Range.

Pick OK.

Note

Trimming a PDF merely conceals material: it won't shrink the file. Put the page size back and the full original material returns.
