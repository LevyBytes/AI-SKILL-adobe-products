## Browser extension won’t print PDFs

### When the print extension fails, here's how to adjust the settings in your browser.

A browser's print extension is what lets you send web pages or documents to the printer from inside the browser. Settings that aren't configured correctly can leave that extension unable to print pages.

### Incorrect Google Chrome settings

Into the web browser's address bar, enter the path chrome://settings/content/pdfDocuments

Beneath Default behavior, pick Open PDFs in Chrome.

With the 'Open PDFs in Chrome' option picked, PDF files load in a fresh Chrome tab.

With the 'Open PDFs in Chrome' option picked, PDF files load in a fresh Chrome tab.

Refresh your PDF and give printing another try.

### Incorrect Microsoft Edge settings

Into the Edge browser's address bar, enter the path edge://settings/content/pdfDocuments

Switch off the toggle for the Always download PDF files setting.

Switching off the 'Always download PDF files' setting means PDFs load in a fresh Edge tab rather than being downloaded.

## PDF doesn’t print

### Learn what to do if your PDF fails to print.

### Printer not connected

Check that the cable joining your printer and computer is firmly seated. Should the printer run through a USB hub, pull the cable off the hub and connect it straight to a USB port on the computer.

Another thing to try is powering the printer off. Give it 30 seconds, switch it back on, and then attempt the PDF print once more.

### Printer drivers not updated

To keep pace with the newest software, including fresh releases of Acrobat Reader and Acrobat, manufacturers routinely refresh their drivers.

Pin down the model number of your printer.

Visit the manufacturer's site and look up drivers or printer drivers.

Track down and download the driver that matches your model number.

Stick to the manufacturer's directions to install the driver.

Reboot your system and have another go at printing the PDF.

## Disrupted transaction between payment and signature

### How to wrap up signing your agreement when the workflow breaks down after the payment has been taken.

Two distinct stages make up the e-signature flow that includes payment:

- Capturing the payment
- Finishing the act of signing

By design, this sequence prevents an agreement from being finalized until its payment has been captured. Should something go wrong at the capture stage, the rest of the flow can stall.

### Signature workflow is disrupted

Use the original link you were given to bring the agreement back up.

- When a message tells you the payment went through and was captured before the breakdown, choose OK and carry on with signing.
- When no such confirmation appears, double-check the payment details and try submitting the payment once more.

## Received file conversion error

### How to clear up errors that surface while you're converting PDFs.

### Browser not compatible

Move to a browser that's supported and have another go at converting the PDF. These are the browsers that work with the Adobe Acrobat Export PDF feature: Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, Google Chrome, and Safari 11.

Read more about Adobe Acrobat Export PDF system requirements.

### Browser cache issues

Empty the browser's cache, then attempt the PDF conversion once more. Cache-clearing steps are covered for each of these:

- Microsoft Edge
- Mozilla Firefox
- Google Chrome
- Safari

### Incorrect firewall or proxy settings

Look over your firewall or proxy configuration and confirm it isn't blocking the upload path to the Internet. How you adjust those firewall or proxy settings differs from one antivirus product and operating system to the next. Follow the procedure that matches your particular antivirus program and OS.

### Persistent browser issues

Have another go at the conversion, this time in a different web browser.

### File size and complexity

Confirm the file you're converting comes in at 100 MB or less. Files above 100 MB are rejected by Adobe Acrobat Export PDF. A file that stays under 100 MB can still prove too dense to convert when it packs in lots of pages, fonts, and graphics, which may push the service past its time-out.

## Payments disabled after modifying an agreement

### How to fix payment that gets switched off once an agreement has been edited.

When the original first recipient hands off their authority to sign, or the sender edits the agreement after that hand-off, payment can end up skipped, so the agreement reaches completion without any payment being secured.

Head to Acrobat Sign and sign in with your admin credentials.

Select Account Settings > Send Settings.

Within the Modify Agreements section, tick the Allow senders to modify agreements checkbox.

Granting senders permission to modify agreements lets them edit an agreement even once delegation has occurred, all without breaking payment processing.

Granting senders permission to modify agreements lets them edit an agreement even once delegation has occurred, all without breaking payment processing.

## Text is garbled in a converted file

### How to keep text from coming out garbled once a PDF has been converted.

### Source PDF quality is low

A PDF you're exporting may convert at low quality if it began life in Microsoft Word and was then turned into a PDF by way of Print to PDF or Scan to PDF.

To get a high-quality result, run the document through Convert to PDF first, then export it.

From the Acrobat on the web homepage, select Convert > Convert to PDF.

Pick Add a file from your device and browse to the file you want to upload.

When the PDF has finished, select Convert from the top menu.

Beneath Export a PDF to, pick the format you want, then select Convert to [selected format].

### Scan quality is low

Pages that come out skewed, smudged, or marked make scanned documents hard to convert with any accuracy. For PDFs of high quality, scan the document over again and confirm the pages sit straight and clean before you reach for Export a PDF once more.

## Legal Templates feature retirement on Adobe Acrobat

### Find answers to common questions surrounding the end of the Legal Templates feature on Adobe Acrobat.

As of January 10, 2025, Adobe Acrobat will stop offering the Legal Templates feature. For anyone on Acrobat for desktop, be aware that this feature could become inaccessible as soon as December 5, 2024.

Should you need the Legal Templates feature in the window between December 5, 2024, and January 10, 2025, reach it straight from Acrobat on the web.

Every document you finish and save by way of Legal Templates on or before January 10, 2025, stays available as a PDF in your Adobe cloud storage. On Acrobat on desktop they live under Your files; on Acrobat on the web, check the Documents area.

Templates left in draft, not completed and saved within Acrobat, will be gone after January 10th. Keeping access to those in-progress drafts in Acrobat means finishing them and saving them as PDFs ahead of January 10.

Past January 10, unlimited tailored legal documents remain something you can create through Rocket Lawyer, our template partner.
