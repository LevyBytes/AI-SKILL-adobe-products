## Custom field validation overview

### Get acquainted with the custom field validations used in e-signature agreements.

As you build agreements for e-signature, custom field validations are yours to add. Their job is to tighten up how data gets collected, making sure what's entered lines up with the criteria you've set.

### Diverse field types

A wide range of field types is yours to drop into the agreements — signatures and initials, text, dates, numbers, checkboxes, dropdown menus, images, links, stamps, and the rest. Having that spread of field types lets you shape agreements around particular needs and requirements, be it pulling in must-have information or securing consent in one form or another.

### Tailored data entry

With custom field validations, you get to pin down exactly which kind of data each field will accept.

Take the Date field: you can set which date format it accepts, give the field a name, prefill dates, lock its values, and even dictate text formats. Text fields work much the same way, lending themselves to defined text formats, character types, ceilings on character length, and more.

## E-signature limitations

Get acquainted with where the Request e-signature feature in Acrobat on the web falls short.

Here's what the Request e-signatures feature can't currently do:

- Text tag field conversion: Fields written in text tag format won't work. That said, text tag processing still runs through the Acrobat Sign template. Build an Acrobat Sign template out of a document that holds a text tag, and you can then turn that template into an agreement.
- Agreement level notes: The ability to attach notes to an agreement is gone from the interface, and there are no plans to bring it back.
- Acrobat Sign template data download: There's no way to pull down the data from an Acrobat Sign template. If you need that template data, either turn to the Create agreements feature on Acrobat on desktop or move up to a paid subscription plan.
- Access to legacy drafts: The Request e-signatures tool won't open legacy drafts. To make use of one, convert it into an Acrobat Sign template that future agreements can draw on.

## Delete agreements

### Learn how to delete agreements.

The Acrobat on the web tools let you safely clear out agreements you no longer want or that have gone stale from your account. That keeps your document workspace tidy and free of clutter.

Note

Deleting an agreement sets off a 'soft deletion.' In practice the agreement shifts into a Deleted queue and stays parked there for 14 days. Once those days pass, it and every document tied to it vanish from the database for good. Any time inside that 14-day window, you can pull the agreement back out of the Deleted folder.

From the Acrobat on the web homepage, choose Documents.

Beneath Files, choose Shared by you.

Pick out the agreement you'd like to delete.

Over in the right pane under Actions, choose Delete .

A dialog box opens; in it, choose Delete anyway.

## Manage bulk sends

### Learn how to view and manage the agreements sent for signature in bulk.

Open Documents from the Acrobat on the web homepage.

In the left pane, choose Bulk sends.

Pick an agreement out of the bulk-sent list to bring up its details.

At the top of the right pane that appears, review these agreement details:

- Title of the agreement
- Date the Bulk Send was sent to participants
- Email address of the userID used to create the Bulk Send
- Overall status of the Bulk Send
- Message included in the initial email
- CCed parties (listed by email address)

Whichever you need, trigger it from the Actions menu in the right pane:

- Open Bulk Send: Brings up the Bulk Send template document in view-only mode, with none of its fields editable.
- Edit Bulk Send: Brings up the Bulk document so you can edit it.
- Cancel: Calls off the Bulk Send.
- Download PDF: Saves the PDF to your device.
- Download Error Report: Pulls down a CSV holding the field-level content for every completed child agreement that came out of this parent Bulk Send.
- Download Individual Documents: When several files went into building the web form, this lets users grab each PDF on its own.
- Hide Bulk Send: Takes the Bulk Send off your Manage page view.
- Reminders: Queue a nudge for yourself or for anyone who has yet to sign.
- Share: Hands the Bulk Send and its status to whatever user email you supply. A recipient can view shared web forms on the Manage page but cannot edit them.
- Add Notes: Attach a private note of your own to the Bulk Send document.

Open the Activity drop-down menu for a time-ordered record of the enablement steps performed on the Bulk Send document.

When a Bulk Send carries multiple agreements, the Agreements section reports their status. Each entry below tallies how many child agreements sit in that state:

- All: Shows every child agreement that the Bulk Send (parent template) produced.
- In Progress: Surfaces the child agreements still waiting on signatures from participants.
- Canceled: Covers agreements the sender called off or that a participant declined.
- Completed: Rounds up the agreements every participant has finished signing.
- Waiting for You: Flags agreements that still need the sender's countersignature.

Note

Any status with zero child agreements is hidden, which means you might see fewer than five of them.

## Recover deleted agreements and templates

### Learn how to recover deleted agreements and templates.

Note

Anything you delete lands in the Deleted queue and stays recoverable for 14 days. Once that window closes, the agreement and its related documents are gone for good.

Open Documents from the Acrobat on the web homepage.

In the left pane, choose Deleted.

Pick the agreement or template you'd like to bring back.

In the right pane, under Actions, choose Restore.

The agreement returns to your agreement list and a success message shows up.
