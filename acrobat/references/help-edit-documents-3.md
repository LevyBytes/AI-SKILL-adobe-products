## Delete pages in PDFs

### See how Acrobat on desktop makes it quick to pull pages you don't want out of a PDF.

Stripping out pages clears away surplus material yet preserves the file's framework along with interactive pieces such as bookmarks and links.

Before you begin

Confirm the PDF can actually be edited: head to File > Document Properties > Security and look over whatever restrictions are listed.

With the PDF open, choose All tools > Organize pages.

Thumbnails for each page show up in the document area.

For a single page, hover its thumbnail and pick Delete from the context menu.

When several pages are involved, work through the Pages Selected field. Two routes are open to you:

- Type out page numbers split by commas, like 2, 4, or give a span such as 6–8.
- Or settle on one of the dropdown's choices.

Note

At least one page has to stay behind — Acrobat won't let you wipe out every page.

From the left pane, choose Delete pages.

Confirm by choosing OK.

Save the PDF.

## Move or copy pages within PDFs

### See how Adobe Acrobat handles moving and duplicating pages inside a single PDF.

Acrobat's visual thumbnail layout makes reshuffling pages or cloning material inside one PDF straightforward. Pages slide around or copy over to a different spot whether you drag them or work from the context menu.

With the PDF open in Acrobat, choose All tools > Organize pages.

Thumbnails for the pages turn up in the document area.

Click the thumbnail of whichever page you intend to move or copy.

For more than one page at a time, hold Ctrl (Windows) or Command (macOS) as you click the thumbnails.

Relocating pages is as simple as dragging the chosen thumbnails to a fresh spot.

Tip

There's also the option to move or copy pages by choosing Page thumbnails from the right pane.

Cutting and pasting goes like this: hover a thumbnail, choose More > Cut. Next, click in the gap between thumbnails where it belongs — a blue line flags the position — and choose Paste from the context menu.

Copying and pasting works much the same: hover a thumbnail, choose More > Copy. Next, click in the gap between thumbnails where it belongs — a blue line flags the position — and choose Paste from the context menu.

Drag thumbnails or lean on the context menu to reorder, cut, or copy the pages of a PDF.

Reorder, cut, or copy PDF pages either by dragging their thumbnails or through the context menu.

Save the PDF.

## Move or copy pages between two PDFs

### See how Acrobat on desktop lets you shift or copy pages out of one PDF and into another.

Shuttling pages from one PDF to a second one happens through a plain drag-and-drop setup. It's an efficient way to reorganize or merge material without putting the files through any conversion.

### Windows

With both PDFs open in Acrobat.

Choose Menu > Window > Tile > Vertically so the two sit beside each other.

Within one PDF, choose All tools > Organize pages.

Thumbnails for each page show up in the document area.

Pick out the page or pages you mean to move or copy:

- Click a single thumbnail for one page.
- Hold Ctrl and click to gather several pages.

Now move or copy what you picked:

- To move: drag the chosen thumbnails over to where you want them in the second PDF.
- To copy: right-click the chosen thumbnails, choose Copy, then right-click the target spot in the other PDF and choose Paste.

Drop a copied page into your PDF by right-clicking in the space between thumbnails and choosing Paste from the menu.

To paste a copied page, right-click between the thumbnails in your PDF and choose Paste from the menu.

For both PDFs, choose File > Save.

### macOS

## Rotate pages in PDFs

### How to spin pages within a PDF so their orientation is corrected or the layout suits your needs.

Launch the PDF, then pick All tools > Organize pages.

Pick one of these methods to set which pages get rotated:

- Open the dropdown beneath PAGES SELECTED and pick whichever option fits.
- Pick a ready-made choice — Even pages, Odd pages, or All pages.
- Type the page numbers yourself.

Set which way the pages turn:

- Rotate page left turns the page 90° counterclockwise.
- Rotate page right turns the page 90° clockwise.

Getting a PDF ready to send out or export? Reach for the Organize Pages tool when you need to pick, reorder, and divide particular pages.

Getting a PDF ready to send out or export? Reach for the Organize Pages tool when you need to pick, reorder, and divide particular pages.

Dismiss the tools pane, then save the PDF.

Note

For a view change that doesn't stick, head to View > Rotate view and pick Rotate view right or Rotate view left. Reopening the PDF later brings back its original page orientation.

## Replace pages in PDFs

### How to swap out pages in PDFs with Acrobat on the desktop.

Pages in a PDF — one or several — can be swapped for pages pulled from a different PDF. That comes in handy when you're refreshing a document or merging material from several sources. The swap touches only the words and pictures on the original pages; interactive bits such as links and bookmarks stay intact.

### Replace pages in a single PDF

Launch the PDF holding the pages you intend to swap, then pick All tools > Organize pages.

Over in the left pane, pick Replace.

When the file explorer window appears, pick the PDF that holds the replacement pages and pick Open.

Within the Replace Pages dialog box:

- Beneath Original, type the page numbers in your current document that should be swapped out.
- Beneath Replacement, type the first page number of the incoming range. Acrobat works out the final page from however many pages are being swapped.

Name both the original page range and the replacement page range to swap out PDF pages.

Name both the original page range and the replacement page range to swap out PDF pages.

Pick Ok, then save the PDF you just revised.

### Replace pages between multiple PDFs

Launch both the PDF you plan to revise and the PDF holding the replacement pages.

For each PDF, pick All tools > Organize pages so the page thumbnails show.

Within the PDF that holds the replacement pages, pick whichever page(s) you want to use:

- Under Windows, hold Ctrl while picking the pages.
- Under macOS, hold Command while picking the pages.

Pull the chosen thumbnails over to the document area of the PDF you're targeting.

Let go of the mouse once the pointer sits squarely on the page number where the new pages belong.

Note

However many pages you chose will take the place of an equal count in the target PDF, beginning at the page number where you dropped them.

Save the PDF you just revised.

## Renumber pages in PDFs

### How to revise or tailor the page numbers in your PDF so they line up with the document's structure and reading flow gets easier.

What shows up in thumbnails and the navigation toolbar won't always agree with the numbers printed on the page. By default the numbering runs in order from 1, yet PDFs frequently open with front matter — think a cover page or a table of contents.

So the shown numbers track your document's structure, give introductory pages Roman numerals, body pages plain numbers, and tack on prefixes for chapter-based schemes. As an illustration, 1-1, 1-2 for Chapter 1 alongside 2-1, 2-2 for Chapter 2.

Pick All tools > Organize pages > Page labels.

Once the Page labels dialog box appears, name a page range.

Pick one of these:

- Begin new section: Kicks off a fresh numbering run. Pick a style, type the starting number, and tack on a prefix if you like.
- Extend numbering used in preceding section to selected pages: Carries the numbering forward from earlier pages and leaves the sequence unbroken.

Tailor PDF page numbering by kicking off a fresh sequence, stretching one further, or carrying it on from earlier pages.

Tailor PDF page numbering by kicking off a fresh sequence, stretching one further, or carrying it on from earlier pages.

Pick OK to put it into effect.

Note

All the Page labels tool touches are the labels shown in thumbnails and page navigation — it won't print page numbers onto the page. When you want numbers visible on the document itself, turn to the Header and footer tool.

## Split PDFs

### How to break a PDF apart into smaller standalone files by page, by bookmark, or by size.

A single PDF or several can be broken into a set of smaller PDFs. As you do, the dividing point can hinge on a page-count ceiling, a file-size ceiling, or the top-level bookmarks.

Launch the PDF, then pick All tools > Organize pages.

Over in the left pane, pick Split.

Open the Split by dropdown and pick how the document should be divided:

- Number of pages: Carves the PDF into smaller documents that each carry a fixed page count.
- File Size: Produces several PDFs at a file size you name.
- Top level bookmarks: Breaks the PDF wherever its top-level bookmarks fall.

Break your PDF into several documents by naming the page count, the file size, or the top-level bookmarks.

Break your PDF into several documents by naming the page count, the file size, or the top-level bookmarks.

When several PDFs need dividing, pick Split multiple files, pick Add files, and pick whichever PDFs you want to split.

Pick Output options to name:

- The destination folder for the divided files.
- The naming convention for the fresh PDFs.

Pick Split.

## Extract pages from PDFs

### How to pull specific pages out of a PDF and build them into a fresh document with Acrobat on the desktop.

Pulling pages out lets you repurpose chosen material from one PDF inside a new document. Everything on the pulled pages carries over — content, form fields, comments, and links from the original. The pulled pages can stay put in the source document, or you can take them out as part of the extraction.

Before you begin

Confirm whether the PDF can be edited. Head to File > Document Properties > Security tab to see any restrictions in place.

Launch the PDF, then pick All tools > Organize pages.

The page thumbnails show up in the document area.

To remove single pages, pick the page thumbnail and then pick Extract pages over in the left pane.

When several pages are involved, lean on the PAGES SELECTED field to name which pages come out. You can type the number yourself or pick an option from the dropdown.

Pick Extract pages over in the left pane.

Within the pop-up box that shows up, pick from these:

- Delete pages after extracting: Takes the pulled pages out of the source PDF.
- Extract pages as separate files: Builds a standalone PDF for each pulled page.
- Leave both unticked to keep the original whole and spin up a new PDF.

Pick the Extract pages options to take pages out, build standalone PDFs, or hold on to originals while spinning up a new PDF from the pages you chose.

Pick the Extract pages options to take pages out, build standalone PDFs, or hold on to originals while spinning up a new PDF from the pages you chose.

Pick Extract.

Note

A new document is where the pulled pages land. Anything tied to pages by way of bookmarks or article threads won't come along.

Save the new PDF whenever you're prompted.

## Crop pages in PDFs

### How to trim pages in PDFs with Acrobat on the desktop.

Trimming lets you cut away unwanted margins or zero in on particular material inside a PDF page. Everyone gets the basic trimming choices in Acrobat, whereas the advanced ones — tweaking TrimBox or BleedBox among them — show up only in Acrobat Pro.

Pick Edit from the top toolbar.

Pick Crop page over in the left pane.

Pull a rectangle across the area you mean to keep. Fine-tune that cropping rectangle by pulling its corner handles.

Double-click within the cropping rectangle.

Adjust the page box settings, page size, page range, and margins over in the Set Page Boxes dialog.

Adjust the page box settings, page size, page range, and margins over in the Set Page Boxes dialog.

Within the Set Page Boxes dialog box, tweak whichever of these settings you need:

- Show All Boxes: Each box gets its own outline color — black for CropBox, red for ArtBox, green for TrimBox, blue for BleedBox. Where boxes overlap, a single line shows.

  - CropBox: the region that prints or stays visible.
  - ArtBox (Acrobat Pro): the principal content zone, white space and all.
  - TrimBox (Acrobat Pro): how big the page ends up once trimmed.
  - BleedBox (Acrobat Pro): the stretch past the trim meant for professional printing.

- Constrain Proportions: Holds every margin equal.
- Remove White Margins: Pares white space down to the artwork.
- Set To Zero: Clears all margins back to nothing.
- Revert To Selection: Goes back to the crop area you picked before.

Want these settings on further pages? Name the page range or pick All beneath Page Range.

Pick OK.

Note

Trimming a PDF merely conceals material — it won't shrink the file. Put the page size back and the full original material returns.

## Add watermarks to PDFs

### How to drop text or image watermarks onto your PDFs with Acrobat on the desktop.

Picture a watermark as set text or an image laid over a PDF to flag ownership, show branding, or label material as confidential. Stamps sit on top, but watermarks get baked into the page. One watermark or several can go onto particular pages across one PDF or many. When the watermark is an image, transparent PNGs give you the cleanest outcome.

Note

The only image types that work as watermarks are PDF, JPEG, and BMP.

Launch the PDF you mean to watermark, then pick Edit.

Pick Watermark > Add over in the left pane.

Within the Add Watermark dialog box, pick your watermark choices:

- Source: Pick Text for a text watermark, or File for an image.
- Appearance: Tune opacity, rotation, and size.
- Position: Fix where it sits vertically and horizontally.

Tip

Lean on the Appearance settings to govern when and how the watermark turns up — tune its visibility, lock the size from page to page, or resize it by hand or against the page size.

To put the watermark across several files, pick Apply to Multiple Files, add your PDFs, pick OK, and pick where they save within the Output Options dialog.

Pick OK.

Lean on the watermark settings panel to tune font, size, rotation, opacity, and page range — then preview and lay the watermark onto one PDF or many.

Lean on the watermark settings panel to tune font, size, rotation, opacity, and page range — then preview and lay the watermark onto one PDF or many.

## Add headers and footers to PDFs

### Lay tailored headers and footers across your PDFs with Adobe Acrobat.

Headers and footers can carry page numbers, dates, titles, and other text. Push them across one PDF or many, fit the margins to your layout, and preview or revise them before you wrap up. Header and footer settings can be saved and reused on other documents.

### Add a header and footer

Launch the PDF, then pick Edit from the global bar.

Pick Header and footer > Add over in the left pane.

Once the dialog box appears, type whatever text you want into the header and footer fields.

Lean on the Font section to tune font type, size, and color.

Lean on the Margin (inches) section to fix the top, bottom, left, and right margins to suit.

Lean on the Add Header and Footer dialog box to drop in text and other details.

Lean on the Preview section to see how the header and footer turn out.

Note

Header and footer settings can be kept for later by picking Save Settings. To reuse the same header and footer settings on other PDFs, pick Apply to Multiple Files > Add files. After that, pick your files, fix output preferences in the Output Options dialog box, and pick OK.

Pick OK.

### Add multiple headers and footers

Several headers or footers can go in so details like page numbers, titles, and dates land in different sections of one document. That can sharpen clarity and consistency when a document gets complex.

Pick Header and footer > Add.
