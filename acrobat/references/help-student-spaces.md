## Student Spaces in Acrobat FAQ

Common questions about working with Student Spaces in Adobe Acrobat, answered here.

Living inside Acrobat on the web, Student Spaces is an AI-driven workspace that gathers a student's materials, supports learning across many formats, and brings classmates together — everything sitting in a single location. Think of each Student Space as a hub built around one topic or task: you can pull in several files at once, spin up study tools such as flashcards and quizzes, back up answers with citations, and pass the whole thing along to others.

For students, the payoff is studying more efficiently, getting ready for exams, and putting together polished presentations without much fuss. Group work is covered too — notes can be swapped, ideas batted around, and team projects tackled together.

What Student Spaces lets you do:

- Keep as many as 100 files viewable and organized in a single location.
- Lean on a ready-made AI Assistant that plays the role of a personal tutor and nudges you toward your objectives.
- Pick up material across formats and build study aids — flashcards, quizzes, study guides, video summaries — that speed up review and help it stick.
- Confirm AI-generated answers against clear, easy-to-read citations.
- Jot down notes to record what you're thinking or to keep a handy AI response.
- Hand your whole Student Space to friends, notes and AI Assistant included.

The intended audience is higher-education students and early-career learners aged 18 or above.

There's no charge for Student Spaces. Unlocking premium features — Podcast, Presentation, and Video summary — calls for a free Adobe account.

Any web or mobile browser will get you into Student Spaces in Acrobat on the web.

A test drive of the basic features is possible without signing in. Set up a personal Adobe account and the standard plus premium study tools open up, along with the ability to share your Student Spaces.

Note

At present, accounts purchased by an institution can't run Student Spaces. Working from an institutional account means you'll have to spin up a free personal Adobe account to use it. Already on AIA for EDU, Creative Cloud Student & Teacher Edition (STE), or Acrobat Pro for Student & Teacher Edition? Then Student Spaces shows up in your account. Wider support for institution-owned accounts is on the way — look back later for news.

For now, Student Spaces ships worldwide in English, across the countries where Adobe products and services are sold. Additional languages and broader regional coverage are coming.

Watch for updates where you are. The following places fall outside the availability of Adobe products and services, AI Assistant for Acrobat among them: Belarus, China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Syria, the Crimean Peninsula, Donetsk People's Republic (DPR), and Luhansk People's Republic (LPR) regions of Ukraine.

A whole spread of study tools comes with Student Spaces, each suited to a different learning style. Run one on its own, or stack several together depending on what you're trying to master.

Reach for text-based study tools — flashcards, quizzes, study guides, study packets, and mind maps — to go over concepts and gauge how well you've got them. For audio and visual review, plus a way to arrange ideas for sharing, turn to multimedia study tools such as podcasts, video summaries, and presentations.

Drop a source into Student Spaces — a document, a web link, some copied text — and citations show up both in AI Assistant responses and in the text of any study tools it generates. Click a citation and it points you to precisely where that detail sits in the source documents, which makes checking facts or digging deeper a simple matter.

Note

Citations won't be available unless a source has been uploaded.

Curated Student Spaces exist to show you the ropes while you engage with relevant, handpicked material tied to topics that matter to students inside and outside the classroom.

Put together by the Adobe Education team, these ready-to-go Student Spaces make a fine way to surface fresh ideas and insights.

What Curated Student Spaces are good for:

- Browsing and reading expertly assembled content
- Picking up ideas on applying Student Spaces in all kinds of situations, from exam prep to chasing your next internship
- Pulling down documents so they're easy to reference
- Coming away with useful insights, a creative spark, and stronger learning

Sharing study materials and collaborating runs smoothly in Student Spaces thanks to the access levels and permissions you set. As you share a Student Space, three preset access levels are on offer for the people you invite:

- Contributor: lets the person comment, drop in artifacts, and handle files and notes — adding them or taking them away.
- Reviewer: the person sees the content and may comment, yet has no power to alter files or notes.
- View Only: the person merely looks at files and notes, with no editing and no commenting allowed.

The default share settings are yours to adjust — open the Student Space up to your whole organization, or lock it down as private. On top of that, anyone signed in can pass a Student Space along again, which keeps collaboration fluid and access in check. Learn how to collaborate with your classmates on Student Spaces.

The generative AI models behind Acrobat's features are not trained on customer content by Adobe. See the way Adobe safeguards what you upload by handling and using content responsibly.

Generating, uploading, or sharing anything that infringes someone else's copyright is off-limits. Where you think your content might step on a third party's copyright, consult a lawyer.

To weigh in on an AI-generated response, hit Dislike at the foot of that response. Pick one reason or several, and tack on extra comments if you'd like.

## Create Student Spaces

Build focused learning hubs out of your own materials with Student Spaces in Acrobat.

Drowning in a heavy or multi-subject course load? Student Spaces in Adobe Acrobat takes tangled materials and scattered notes and reshapes them into tidy, topic-level study plans. Feed in your materials and structured guides, flashcards, quizzes, and a clear learning path come together fast. Less effort hunting for a starting point, more of it spent learning with confidence.

Head to Student Spaces, pick Create Student Space, and kick things off one of these two ways:

- Start with your sources: drag notes, slides, PDFs, readings, or links onto the chat box — or browse to them through the Add sources option — to upload.
- Start with a topic: type into the chat box the subject you'd like to study together with the study tool to apply. For example, "Create a study guide on World History."

Built around whatever topic you asked for, the Student Space pulls in any sources you contribute or a study tool produced from that topic.

The Overview page is where you add sources, kick off studying with quick prompts, and produce study tools for your Student Space.

The Overview page is where you add sources, kick off studying with quick prompts, and produce study tools for your Student Space.

Turn to Chat when you want the AI tutor's tailored guidance. Raise questions about study tactics, core concepts, or plainer explanations — for instance:

- How should I best go about this?
- Where do the most important parts lie?
- What's the key thing to take away here?

Look over citations to trace where information originates. Open the sections they cite to revisit important passages and dig into related context across your materials.

Note

Citations show up only once a source file has been uploaded. With no source added, none will appear.

Hang on to handy responses as notes via the Save to notes option sitting at the bottom of each response.

Tip

Grab key insights, tweak them where needed, and put them back to use for outlines, drafts, or study plans.

## Get trusted answers with AI citations

See how the AI tutor inside Student Spaces helps you make sense of tough topics through explanations rooted in your own study materials.

Slogging through demanding academic material, a fast answer on its own falls short. You also have to know where that answer originated and whether it's trustworthy enough for studying, assignments, or exams.

Student Spaces in Acrobat helps you make sense of tough topics with AI-generated explanations plainly anchored in your own study material, paired with citations that link straight back to the source. The result: AI explanations that read clearer, hold up better, and are safer to lean on for academic work like exam prep, note-taking, and proofreading.

Open a Student Space you already have to begin working through your study material, or spin up a fresh one to get going.

Through Chat you can pose questions in natural, study-style prompts, such as:

- Boil this 20‑page PDF down into bullet points.
- Pull out the key takeaways from this chapter for me.
- Across this reading, what are the 5 main arguments?
- Walk me through this as though the topic were brand new to me.

Where it helps, have the AI tutor recast or trim the content in study-friendly ways, for example:

- Recast this article in plain English.
- Sum up each section within 2 sentences.
- Sort this reading into 3 main themes.
- Hand me an executive-summary version.

Note

That's especially useful for unpacking abstract concepts, multi-step theories, and thick academic wording.

Click a source marker inside an AI explanation to land on the precise section of the original document and confirm the detail.

## Text-based study tools

Reshape your materials into focused study formats using the text-based tools in Student Spaces.

What text-based study tools do is convert your notes and readings into concentrated formats built for comprehension, practice, and exam prep — so organizing eats up less of your time and learning gets more of it.

Your notes and readings can become AI-powered flashcards, quizzes, study guides, study packets, and mind maps, all reinforcing how well you grasp and hold onto the material.

### Why use text-based study tools

Text-based study tools earn their keep when your aim is to:

- Grasp key concepts and sum them up
- Drill your recall and gauge how ready you are
- Carve big subjects into pieces you can handle
- Walk into exams feeling prepared

Tip

Once text-based study tools have helped the concepts click, branch out to multimedia tools that reinforce learning through audio, video, and presentations.

These tools are reachable from the Student Spaces homepage or from the Overview page inside any Student Space. Drop in your material, settle on a tool, and whatever gets generated lands in the Study Tools section, set to use.

### Mind map

What the Mind map tool does is recast your study material as a visual knowledge structure, sorting topics and subtopics into a hierarchical knowledge tree so the whole picture comes together in one place. A Mind map, once generated, shows up as a tile in the Study Tools section. Open that tile and your content appears as linked ideas within a single visual view.

Ways to explore content

- Click any topic to go further into the ideas tied to it
- Pause your cursor over a topic and pick Explain, and Chat lays out a clear breakdown of it
- Trace the citations back to the original sources that underpin the information

Viewing and navigation options

- Move the zoom in or out to home in on particular topics
- Pull the canvas around to travel across the map
- Flip between layouts to see the relationships laid out in other visual forms

## Collaborate with your classmates on Student Spaces

See how to hand a Student Space to your classmates, or step into one someone has already opened up to you, so the studying happens together.

Bringing classmates into your Student Space, or accepting an invite into theirs, is the quick way to pool both your materials and your study tools. Send an invite over a link or by email so others can look over notes, try out the study tools, and drop in their own content alongside yours, regardless of whether they sign in or come in as a guest. What stays private is the chat history, which means each person keeps their focus on the materials and works through them at whatever pace suits them.

### Share a Student Space with classmates

Pull up whichever Student Space the two of you will be working in.

From the menu along the top, hit Share.

When the dialog box comes up, hit Change beneath Link access, set both the link's access level and what permission the recipients get, and then hit Done.

- Anyone in <your‑organization> with the link (Default option for enterprise users)
- Invited people only

- Contributors: Can add comments, and add or remove files and notes
- Reviewers: Can add comments
- Viewers: Can view files and notes

Get a Student Space out to others and set its link access before the invites go out.

Get a Student Space out to others and set its link access before the invites go out. Note

Decide which sharing route fits:

- Invite people: Type in who you're sending to by name or email, look over the message, then hit Send.
- Direct link share: Hit Copy link to share, then pass that copied link along to the people you have in mind.
- Share via third-party apps: Pick whichever supported third-party app you like — WhatsApp, GroupMe, Discord, Reddit, or LinkedIn — and do what the onscreen prompts tell you.

## Create flashcards and quizzes in Student Spaces

See how Student Spaces in Acrobat takes what you're reading and spins it into flashcards and quizzes.

From your notes, chapters, and readings, Student Spaces builds flashcards and quizzes in moments — a fast way to go back over the main ideas, gauge how well you've grasped them, and surface weak spots before they bite. Because every answer ties back to a source citation, your studying stays rooted in your own documents, and you pick whichever format fits each subject, with everything gathered in one place.

Head into Student Spaces in Acrobat and pick a starting point:

- From existing study material: Hit Create Student Space, send in your notes or documents, and over on the Overview page pick Flashcards or Quiz. Want both? Just run through each tool in turn.
- From scratch on a topic: Over on the Student Spaces homepage, pick Flashcards or Quiz, and in the chat panel spell out the topic specifics — subject, say, or difficulty, or how many cards. To get the other tool too, run the step again.

After generation, the flashcards and quizzes show up inside a Student Space, set for you to study.

Pick the flashcards or the quiz that were generated from the Study Tools tab, and dive in:

- Hit Maximize to drop into focus mode
- Go through the questions one by one, working with whichever tool you're in
- On a flashcard, hit Show answer (or tap Space) to flip it over
- On a quiz, mark an answer per question, then hit Submit — that's when your score appears and you can look back over how you did
- Hit Next to advance to the following card in the stack
