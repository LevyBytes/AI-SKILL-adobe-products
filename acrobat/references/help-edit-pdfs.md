## PDF editing overview

### Get acquainted with what Acrobat on the web can do when editing PDFs — reworking text and images right through to annotations and formatting tweaks.

Acrobat on the web lets you handle fast, essential edits straight in the browser, with no need to download and re-upload PDFs, which trims time and keeps the workflow smooth.

### Text edits

- Basic editing: Adding, swapping, or removing text is effortless. Typos get fixed, fonts swapped, typeface changed, and text resized with ease.
- Formatting: Tune text alignment, spacing, and formatting. The tool handles advanced text editing, though intricate layouts may still call for a few manual tweaks.
- Changing fonts: Lean on embedded fonts so your document reads the same from one device to the next, since whether a font is available shapes how text shows up.

### Image edits

- Basic editing: Drop in, swap out, resize, and reposition images inside your PDF.

Tip

When heavier editing is needed, image editing tools such as Adobe Photoshop or Adobe Illustrator are the better route before you bring images into your PDF.

- Performance: Shrink an image before you insert it to keep the document running smoothly, since hefty image files can drag on performance.
- Accessibility tools: Attach alternative text to images and tag them properly so screen readers navigate them more easily.

## Edit images

### Find out how Acrobat on the web lets you rotate, reposition, or remove images.

Right from your browser, images inside your PDFs are simple to edit, keeping your documents tidy and easy on the eyes.

From the Acrobat on the web homepage, choose Edit > Edit text & images.

Pick Select a file and browse your device to upload the PDF file.

Pick the image you want to edit and reach for the tools in the Edit panel to make changes:

- Spin the image clockwise by choosing Rotate right
- Spin the image anti-clockwise by choosing Rotate left
- Get rid of the image by choosing Delete image
- Swap the image by choosing Replace image, then using the dialog box that appears to pick an image.
- Put text on the image by choosing Add text, then dragging the cursor where you want it and typing. Once text is in place, the text tools in the left panel let you reposition or reformat it.
- Shift the image elsewhere by hovering over it until the drag handle icon shows, then dragging the image to its new spot.

The refreshed PDF shows the edited images, and every change syncs automatically into Adobe cloud storage.

As you reposition an image, the horizontal and vertical dotted lines help you line it up precisely.

## Split PDFs

### See how one PDF file gets divided into several PDFs.

Bulky PDFs break down into tidier, smaller pieces with Acrobat on the web. By marking particular pages or ranges, you turn out individual PDF documents quickly.

From the Acrobat on the web homepage, pick Edit > Split a PDF.

Pick Select a file, then browse your device to bring in the PDF you intend to divide.

Tap the dotted horizontal divider lines to flag where the pages should break into separate PDFs.

Tip

Selecting the dotted lines recolors them blue. The page count of your PDF caps you at up to 22 divider lines for splitting it.

Between the page thumbnails you'll spot dotted lines; tap one to call for a split right at that spot.

Pick Continue.

When the Save your split files dialog box appears, tick the Save in a new folder checkbox so the resulting PDFs land in their own folder.

Pick Save.

The PDF divides into several PDFs that go to Adobe cloud storage. Reach them later from either the Recent or the Documents section.
