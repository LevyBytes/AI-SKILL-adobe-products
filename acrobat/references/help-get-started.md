## Workspace basics

### Learn how to navigate and use the Acrobat workspace.

### Know your workspace

Three separate views make up the Acrobat workspace:

- Home view: where your recent files, documents others have shared, and everyday tools are within reach.
- All tools view: a browsable rundown of every tool Acrobat puts at your disposal.
- Document view: the place to handle open files, several of which show up as tabs inside one window.

### Home view

Starting up Acrobat brings you to the Home screen, where you will find these elements:

- News about recently added capabilities so you stay current with what Acrobat now offers.
- Short tutorials that walk you through important features, workflows, pointers, and recommended practices.
- A listing of documents you opened lately, cloud files you own as well as those recently shared with or opened by you.

From the Home view, you can access: A. Application menu B. Create a PDF C. Help D. Notifications E. Adobe account F. All tools G. List view H. Thumbnail view I. Home page menu

From the Home view, you can access: A. Application menu B. Create a PDF C. Help D. Notifications E. Adobe account F. All tools G. List view H. Thumbnail view I. Home page menu

These tabs and buttons appear within the Home view:

- Recent: Lists the files you opened most recently, whether they live on your machine, in Adobe cloud storage, or in another linked storage service. A filter narrows the view to files you shared, files shared with you, or local files, and each item shows its sharing status.
- Starred: shows every file you have marked with a star.
- Chats: opens conversations together with the files shared in them.

  - Your documents: holds all of your files, kept in the cloud.
  - Scans: the documents you have scanned in.
  - Shared by you: the files you sent out to other people.
  - Shared by others: the files other people sent your way.

- Other storage accounts: Reach files held on your machine or in linked web storage services, among them Box, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, and Microsoft SharePoint.
- Third-party apps: hook into meeting apps to pull in meeting transcript features.
- Quick details: Picking a file here brings up a preview of the tools you turn to most often.
- Agreements: Access all your agreements.
- Get notifications: Flags you whenever a shared document changes state, covering requests to view, review, and sign. Check Acrobat notifications for more information.
- Recommended tools: Reach the tools you use regularly straight from the tab on the Home page.
- Manage profile: Edit your account information, adjust your preferences, and log out of your account.
- Search: Search your files.
- Get help: Open acrobat Help and tutorials, send feedback, and find out what features have been added.

### All tools view

Within the All tools view, tools sit in categories so they are simple to reach. Choosing a category lists its individual tools along the left panel, and from there you pick the tool you want to apply to the document that is open.

## Navigate through pages — Move between pages

### Moving from page to page inside Acrobat — here's the how-to.

Launch Acrobat and pick the document you'd like to read.

Select Menu > View > Page navigation (Windows) or View > Page navigation (macOS) and use the navigation menu tools:

- To go to the next page, select Next page from the menu.
- To go to the previous page, select Previous page from the menu.
- To go to the first page, select First page from the menu.
- To go to the last page, select Last page from the menu.
- To go to a specific page, select Go to page from the menu

Moving through a PDF is handled by these page navigation choices. For quick reach, the same navigation buttons are also mirrored along the bottom of the right panel.

### Navigate through pages — Open and navigate an article thread

Think of an article as a guided route through a PDF: it carries the reader along one storyline, leaping past any pages or page regions that fall outside that thread. The experience mirrors how you'd flip through a printed newspaper or magazine, staying with a single piece and passing over everything else. As you follow it along, the view scales up or down so whatever portion of the article you're on takes up the whole screen. Here's how to follow an article thread inside a PDF:

From the Side Panels, open Articles.

A double-click on the Article icon kicks off your reading.

Stepping along the thread is done with Enter, Shift-click, Shift + Enter, or Ctrl-click.

Want out before the thread finishes? With the Hand tool active, press Shift + Ctrl-click, which drops you back to the page view you had before.

## Asian, European, and Middle Eastern language support

### A look at handling PDFs written in Asian, Cyrillic, and right-to-left scripts.

### Asian language PDFs

Documents whose text uses Asian writing systems can be opened, searched, and printed through Acrobat, covering:

- Traditional and Simplified Chinese
- Japanese
- Korean

So that Acrobat features such as form filling, commenting, and digital signatures work with these scripts, put in the matching Asian language font packs.

When a PDF is produced, both PDFMaker and the Adobe PDF printer pull in most Asian fonts on their own and embed them. Which Asian fonts get embedded is something you can manage.

On Windows, opening and printing documents written in Asian languages doesn't require that Asian language support already sit on your machine. Should a PDF call for fonts you don't have, Acrobat prompts you to add them.

### Cyrillic, Central European, and Eastern European language PDFs

PDFs whose fonts are embedded can be opened, edited, and printed in Acrobat across these scripts:

- Cyrillic text, including Bulgarian and Russian
- Central European text
- Eastern European text, including Czech, Hungarian, and Polish

Searching works on these fonts whether or not they're embedded.

Note

Where a PDF relies on fonts for its form fields or text boxes that aren't present on your machine, Acrobat steps in to fetch those missing fonts for you the next time you check for updates.

### Hebrew, Arabic, Thai, and Vietnamese language PDFs

Thai and Vietnamese text are both handled by Acrobat. On the Windows side, Acrobat additionally handles Arabic and Hebrew.

### Right-to-left languages

Acrobat's right-to-left language options surface the interface controls that govern paragraph direction, digit style, and ligatures. Turning the option on lets you pick the writing direction (left-to-right or right-to-left) and the digit style (Western or Arabic-Indic) applied when you build and complete particular form fields, attach digital signatures, and mark up text boxes.

## Work smarter with the prompt bar

From a single spot, lean on plain-language prompts to track down tools, pull up answers, and kick off new work.

On the Acrobat Studio desktop homepage, the prompt bar folds the AI Assistant, search, and the creation features of Acrobat and Adobe Express together into one tidy area. Rather than hunting through menus, you simply state your intent. Type a short, everyday-language prompt and the tool points you toward the matching actions, answers, or tools, converting what you meant into results with fewer steps along the way.

### Unified starting point for your work

Built to act as a smart command surface, the prompt bar gathers your routine tasks in one place so your attention stays on the outcome you're after. Be it digging into a document, producing content, or hunting through your files, the prompt bar clears away friction and shortens the path to a result.

- Ask questions about your documents: Select Doc Q&A, add one or more files, and enter prompts like "Summarize the key takeaways" or "Compare insights across these documents." Learn how to explore your documents using AI Assistant and gain insights across PDF Spaces.
- Access Acrobat tools instantly: Use the Find tools and describe your task to discover the relevant Acrobat tools, such as "Help me combine multiple PDFs into one file" or "Edit text in a PDF."
- Create professional presentations in minutes: Selecting Generate presentation, add a document, and prompt with requests such as "Create a 5‑slide presentation from this report" or "Turn this report into a client‑ready pitch deck with key highlights." Learn how to generate professional presentations.
- Explore ready‑to‑use Adobe Express templates: Select Explore templates and prompt with requests like "Show templates for flyers or invoices" or "Find templates for reports or project summaries." Learn how to create marketing documents using Adobe Express.
- Search across PDFs, PDF Spaces, and Adobe Express files: Use Search files to find documents by keywords in the file name or content, such as "project budget" or "pricing details."

Together these features hand you quicker entry to the tools that are already part of your routine while surfacing new approaches to building and creating.

Use the prompt bar to begin tasks quickly - analyze documents, create visuals, build presentations, or browse templates.

Use the prompt bar to begin tasks quickly - analyze documents, create visuals, build presentations, or browse templates.

### Availability and access

For now the prompt bar ships as a limited release, reaching Acrobat Studio users on both desktop and web and in English alone. Everyone else stays on the current experience until the wider rollout arrives.

Rather than standing in for the Acrobat tools, the prompt bar lays down a faster, smarter route to them, so searching, creating, and exploring happen without breaking your stride.
